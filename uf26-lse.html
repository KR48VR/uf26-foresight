London School of Economics and Political Science · September 2026

LSE’s scarce product was always a way of seeing society. AI makes average social-science output cheap. That is an opening, not an obituary — if the School will take it.

Kramer arrived from Hewlett and Stanford Law with five problems: democracy, political economy, sustainability, inequality, new technology. MacArthur has now paid $2 million for a Global Forum that puts social science in the AI room. A forum is not a faculty incentive system.

Board two-pager

For the President and Council

Crux

Make social science the scarce input to how AI is governed and used — before economics, IR and public policy become promptable commodities, and before a US-style culture war eats the School’s licence to speak.

Diagnosis in six lines

LSE is a specialist social-science school on a tight central-London site whose brand still sorts people into ministries, banks, NGOs and newsrooms. It is not a comprehensive university and should not try to become one. Kramer took office in April 2024 and, in his inaugural lecture, refused to offer solutions: he offered five problems and a sentence from the School’s own past — “what is needed is hard thinking.” That is the right register. The risk is that hard thinking looks, from the outside, like more working papers while foundation-model firms and finance platforms set the terms on which societies actually run. In February 2026 MacArthur awarded $2 million for a Global Forum for AI and the Social Sciences, led by Cosmina Dorobantu and Helen Margetts at the Data Science Institute, with a first meeting in September 2026 on work. Kramer is also building toward a Global Institute that puts social science inside AI’s design loop. Good. Insufficient. Undergraduate admissions remain ferocious — about 29,700 applications for roughly 1,970 places in 2025/26 — and the classroom is one of the most international in Europe. UK levy and visa policy hit that classroom the way they hit Imperial, without Imperial’s lab alternative. Separately, Kramer has already had to tell donors he will not move LSE positions for money. That sentence will be tested again.

Guiding policy

Stop treating AI as a methods elective. Treat it as the environment in which LSE’s five problems now live. Build the Forum and Institute as places that change what departments certify, not as events. Defend the School’s voice without importing an American campus war. Keep the classroom two-facing even if the Home Office does not want it to be.

Coherent actions

  1. Require every undergraduate and taught-graduate programme to name the human judgment it still certifies once a model can draft the policy memo. Publish the list. Close or redesign programmes that cannot.
  2. Put Forum and Institute money into joint appointments that sit between DSI and the old departments — not into a parallel events shop.
  3. A donor and partnership protocol that restates Kramer’s line as Council policy: no position changes for gifts; time spent explaining is allowed; silence after a fight is not.
  4. An international-classroom plan for the levy years: which master’s shrink first, which are protected as the School’s diplomatic objects.

Full report

What LSE is

LSE is a social-science specialist: economics, politics, law, international relations, sociology, method. It sells a way of seeing, and a network, from a site that cannot sprawl. Times and Sunday Times named it UK University of the Year in 2025 and kept it first in 2026. That is reputation. The operating system is still international fees, a ferocious undergraduate filter, and departments that hire as if the American market were next door.

Forces that actually move this institution

  • Cheap analysis. The average brief, regression, and literature review is no longer scarce. The scarce thing is framing a problem a model has not been asked, and standing behind it in a room.
  • STEM capture of the AI conversation. If social science is not in the design loop, LSE becomes a commentator on other people’s infrastructure.
  • UK funding and visas. Same levy and compliance squeeze as the rest of English HE. LSE has less lab overhead and more brand. Brand does not pay the rates on Aldwych.
  • Polarisation as operating environment. Kramer’s Hewlett years were about holding a political community together. He has already said he would not change an LSE position for a donor. London is not Palo Alto. The test will rhyme.
  • City adjacency. Ministries, City, courts, NGOs, media. That is LSE’s live laboratory — if assessment uses it, rather than citing it.

Crux

Turn the five challenges and the MacArthur Forum into a change in what LSE certifies and whom it appoints — so that social science is an input to AI-era government and markets, not a spectator sport. A September convening that leaves departmental finals untouched is a missed year.

Kernel Reconstruction, September 2026 — not LSE’s published plan

Diagnosis

LSE still sorts elites. The product underneath the sort — social-scientific judgment — is being commoditised by the same tools the School is convening forums about.

Guiding policy

Hard thinking as an examined practice. Institute as appointments, not hospitality. Voice without donor veto. International classroom as a protected asset.

Coherent actions

Programme-level certification lists. Joint posts, not parallel events. Donor protocol as Council policy. Levy-years map of which master’s are diplomatic objects. Use the city as a live exam, not a backdrop.

Critical uncertainties

Axis A — Does social-science evidence still shape AI, government and markets? Or do STEM-capital and platforms set the terms.

Axis B — Does LSE remain a two-facing global school? Or become a more British, more cautious London institution.

A in-loop · B global

Aldwych observatory

2030

The MacArthur Forum has produced joint posts between the Data Science Institute and Economics, Government and International Relations — not only a September convening. A taught-master’s capstone is now a live brief for a ministry, a firm or an NGO, defended in the room with the tools off for the framing hour. International students still fill Houghton Street from more than one bloc. Kramer’s five problems show up on reading lists as examined questions, not as a presidential brand.

2040

LSE is the place that taught the non-STEM half of the AI state: how to frame, cost and contest a system other people built. The site is still small. The shadow is large. A hiring panel in a central bank or a foundation can tell an LSE dissertation from a well-prompted policy memo because the viva record travels with it.

A in-loop · B British

Whitehall college

2030

Influence is real and more domestic. The levy and a shorter graduate visa shrink the international master’s that paid the Aldwych rent. The School becomes a national social-science instrument: excellent for UK government, thinner as a world gathering place. A student who wanted a two-passport career starts arranging the second door in Lent term.

2040

Excellent for UK government and for a London professional class. Less of a world gathering place. Foreign faculties still cite a few departments; they no longer treat the MSc as a default second passport. The Forum is a Whitehall seminar series with better catering.

A spectator · B global

Commentary house

2030

The Forum is famous. Departments still examine 2019 memos. Students come for the brand and the city; they write take-home essays a model can draft overnight. Events volume is high. Joint appointments are rare. A capstone can be completed without speaking to anyone who will use the work.

2040

A prestigious commentator. Platforms and finance firms write the rules; LSE writes the op-eds. The undergraduate filter is still ferocious. The product underneath the filter is a well-edited prompt.

A spectator · B British

Quiet school

2030

Both the global classroom and the intellectual franchise thin. International master’s shrink; the Forum becomes a calendar of polite conversations. A student can collect a Houghton Street brand without once defending a frame in a live room.

2040

A well-run specialist college. The century’s arguments moved to wherever the systems are actually designed. LSE remains a good London address and a weaker claim on how societies are run.

Implications

Appointments and exams help in every world except “quiet school.” Events without posts help only “commentary house.” Kramer’s donor line is a load-bearing wall in all four; if it cracks, the School’s licence to speak goes with it.

A bad strategy would be: a glossy Institute, unchanged finals, a quieter public voice after the next donor argument, and hope that “University of the Year” is a strategy.

For students · LSE

A model can write the brief. It cannot sit in the seminar and defend the frame.

You came for a way of seeing. That is still available. It is no longer automatic. If your programme never asks you to think in the room with the tools off, you are buying a postcode.

Best case

Aldwych observatory

Hard seminars. Live policy rooms. A network that still sorts you.

Narrow excellence

Whitehall college

Deeper British doors. Fewer overseas ones. Plan the second passport yourself.

Comfortable trap

Commentary house

Great events. Same essays as a good prompt. Employers will notice.

Worst case

Quiet school

You collected a brand. The arguments moved.

Do this while you are here

  • Take every unseen, oral, and live brief. That is the degree.
  • Attach yourself to a London institution — ministry, court, newsroom, firm, NGO — that will not accept a generated memo.
  • Use DSI and the Forum as instruments, not as a substitute for a department that still marks hard.
  • Keep a working relationship in a second country. LSE’s value was two-facing. So is yours.
