Nanyang Technological University · Scratch rebuild · September 2026

NTU is Singapore’s second comprehensive university: 35 years old, now regularly in the world top 15, with an Imperial-linked medical school and a president who talks about speed. The question is whether “speed” means labs that reach firms faster — or the same committee calendar as NUS, with better AI ranks.

President Ho Teck Hua calls NTU a 35-year-old startup. The College of Computing and Data Science was stood up in months. ShanghaiRanking put NTU first in the world for AI. The campus dropped AI-detector software and issued Google’s premium tools instead. The written plan is that 40 percent of courses across 52 undergraduate programmes will include AI-related learning by 2030. That target is only real if those courses changed the exam, not just the reading list.

Board two-pager

For the President and Board of Trustees

Crux

NTU can outrank NUS on a QS year and still be treated, at home, as the second national university. If it copies NUS’s faculty mix, campus politics and degree shapes, the rank becomes a costume. Stay the other engine: faster to stand up a lab, more willing to shut one, more tightly wired to firms at one-north and in China and ASEAN. A medical school and a few humanities chairs do not make it a second NUS — and should not try to.

Diagnosis in six lines

NTU’s research engine in materials, EEE, AI, communication and sustainability is real. So is the identity problem. It is no longer the upstart on the west side of the island. It is a top-15 global university with LKC Medicine, a business school, and a new honours college. Ho is also founding executive chairman of AI Singapore. That national role helps talent and hurts focus. Google’s stack, not OpenAI’s, is the campus default. Differentiation only if the courses attached to it changed how they examine.

Guiding policy

Stay the fastest serious research university in the system. Use CCDS as a method that infects the other colleges, not as a fortress. Judge NTU2030 on whether non-computing students can work a live problem with a model and a domain, not on course-count toward 40 percent.

Coherent actions

  1. Publish a 2027 audit of the 40-percent target: which courses changed assessment, not just reading lists.
  2. Make Honours College pull CCDS into society and sustainability problems, not another certificate.
  3. Keep hiring Nanyang Assistant Professors as the growth edge.
  4. Build two or three Garage-style firm labs into credit-bearing studios with P&L, not showcases.
  5. Protect a thin but serious humanities and social-science core so “human-centric AI” is not a press phrase.

Start

A 2027 audit of the 40-percent AI target that counts changed exams, not reading lists. Honours College pulling CCDS into live society problems. Two or three Garage-style firm labs as credit-bearing studios with a P&L.

Stop

Course-count as the NTU2030 scoreboard. CCDS as a fortress the other colleges visit. Rankings used as a substitute for translation.

Go

Stay the fastest serious research university in the system. Keep hiring Nanyang Assistant Professors as the growth edge. Protect a thin humanities and social-science core so “human-centric AI” is not a slogan.

Full report

What it is

NTU is Singapore’s second comprehensive research university and the youngest in the world top 15. Engineering and physical sciences are the spine; LKC Medicine, Nanyang Business School and CCDS are the new organs. The Yunnan Garden campus is a single-site advantage NUS does not have.

Forces that actually move this institution

  • AI research lead inside Singapore. ShanghaiRanking GRAS world #1 in AI (2025). QS 2027 holds #12.
  • Ho-era institutional speed. CCDS, May 2024. Honours College. NTU2030 framed around the quality of minds, not the next subject rank.
  • A different vendor stack from NUS. Google premium tools; detectors retired. Differentiation only if pedagogy follows the contract.
  • National machinery. National GRIP with NUS; Ho’s AI Singapore chairmanship. NTU is inside the system it markets itself as outrunning.
“We are like a startup — only 35 years old — we are very agile and we move fast.” — Ho Teck Hua, September 2026

Crux

NTU’s scarce problem is how to stay young after becoming important. Rankings and a college of computing are how young universities announce arrival. They are also how they ossify.

A real · B distinct

The Other Engine

2030

A hiring chair at a semiconductor or climate-tech firm can name three NTU labs they used this year. Undergraduates still sit large lectures, but the last-year project is a firm problem with a named supervisor on both sides. CCDS method is visible in Engineering, Business and LKC Medicine — not only in a computing silo. Rank is high; nobody in the room is talking about it.

2040

NTU is the campus the region calls when something has to be built, not when something has to be blessed. NUS remains the older prestige house. That split is the point. A student who wanted the NUS mix went to Kent Ridge; a student who wanted a lab that ships stayed.

A real · B NUS-west

Second Flagship

2030

NTU has a medical school, a growing arts presence and a strategy deck that reads like NUS with a newer campus. Rank is fine. Faculty meetings sound the same as Kent Ridge. Kill-lists of dead centres do not exist; new centres do.

2040

Employers treat the two degrees as interchangeable. NTU spent twenty years becoming a second copy and is now priced as one. The ‘young’ story is a museum label on a 50-year-old incumbent.

A brand · B distinct

Ranked Specialist

2030

Engineering, materials and AI are excellent and famous. The rest of the university is a support act. International students come for those departments and leave without touching Singapore. LKC Medicine is a thin partnership that never became a full academic medical centre — and never needed to, because the specialist brand was enough.

2040

NTU is a highly ranked specialist institute wearing a comprehensive name. When those three fields cool, the whole brand cools with them. Parents still quote QS. Firms quote three departments.

A brand · B NUS-west

Young Incumbent

2030

Rank is used as proof that nothing needs to change. Committees multiply. ‘Smart Campus’ is a tour route. A student can complete a degree without a named person outside the university on the final-year project.

2040

A 50-year-old university that still calls itself young, with the cost base of an incumbent and the distinctiveness of neither. The second-engine story is a prospectus paragraph.

Implications

The Board should stop using proximity to NUS in QS as a KPI. The useful comparison is time-to-new-unit, share of courses that changed how they examine, and firms that came out of Garage and GRIP.

A bad strategy would be: celebrate the AI rank, count module titles toward 40 percent, and let CCDS become a gated college the rest of the university visits on open days.

For students · NTU

You came because this campus said it was faster. Check whether your course actually is.

NTU’s pitch is that it moves faster than NUS and that its AI labs are already world-class. That only helps you if your final-year project has a named person outside the university, and if “AI-related learning” in your course means a different exam — not a new slide in week two. Rank will not follow you into the interview.

Best case for you

The Other Engine

A hiring manager at a semiconductor, climate-tech or hospital-tech firm can name the NTU lab or studio you worked in. Your last-year project is a firm problem with a supervisor on both sides. You are not explaining the QS rank in the interview. You are walking through the work.

Comfortable trap

Second Flagship

You have a top-15 degree that looks and sounds like NUS on a newer campus. Employers treat the two as interchangeable. You spent four years becoming a second copy and will be priced as one.

Narrow excellence

Ranked Specialist

If you are in computing, materials or AI, the brand is a rocket. If you are not, you are in a support act. International classmates came for those departments and may never have used the rest of the campus.

Worst case

Young Incumbent

The speeches are still about being 35. Committees multiplied. “Smart Campus” is a tour route. You sat a 2019-style exam with a better laptop.

Do this while you are here

  • Go Use the Google AI suite. Then submit one piece of work the suite cannot finish — a lab result, a prototype a firm touched, an oral defence.
  • Start If your final-year project has no named person outside NTU, change it. The Garage, a hospital partner, or a one-north lab is the point of this campus.
  • Start Get into Honours College, Turing AI, CN Yang, or a Garage lab if you can. Those are the rooms where the agility story is still true.
  • Start Cross the College of Computing with a non-computing school at least once. A second computing module is not the same thing.
  • Start Do not collect extra modules to look “more NUS.” NTU’s bet is the other engine. Act like it.
  • Stop Stop treating a rewritten assignment as the AI reform. If the final still marks the finished file, you used NTU as a prompt lab.
