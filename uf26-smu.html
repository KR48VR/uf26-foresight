Board two-pager

For the President and Board of Trustees

Crux

SMU’s bet is the small room and the city job: a class of 40 that makes you speak, and a third-year placement that is part of the degree. Lily Kong has already said the first job is being eaten by software. If the internship stays a scramble and the seminar becomes a lecture with nicer chairs, downtown is just rent — and employers will test a graduate in an afternoon without you.

Diagnosis in six lines

QS subject 2026 called SMU the world’s most improved university. FT Research Insights 2025 put LKCSB 3rd in Asia for research that decision-makers use. ResWORK launched early 2026. The AID framework says adapt assessments, include AI critically, double down on human skills. Unlike NUS, NTU and SUTD, SMU still uses detection as one tool among others. That is a tell. A university that believes in judgement should be able to examine it without software that does not work.

Guiding policy

Own the third-year job. Redesign the first two years so a graduate can enter work at the level AI has not flattened. Make SMU the place Singapore sends people who must decide, not just analyse.

Coherent actions

  1. Retire detection as a governance story. Build oral defence and live case as the SMU signature assessment by AY2028.
  2. Put ResWORK, the Business AI programmes and government capstones on one ledger the Board can see — placements into second-and-third-year work.
  3. Keep the seminar format sacred. Growth that turns SMU into a lecture university kills the only pedagogy that matches the crux.
  4. Hire a visible cluster in AI governance, labour and evaluation — not another generic analytics group.
  5. Use the Bras Basah campus as a civic studio with the state and firms, not as a downtown amenity.

Full report

What it is

SMU is Singapore’s specialist university for management, social sciences, law and computing-in-context. It is small on purpose, downtown on purpose, interactive on purpose. SMU2030’s line is “shape impact and transform lives.” That only means something if the graduate can do work a model cannot.

Forces that actually move this institution

  • Collapse of the entry-level knowledge-work rung — Kong’s third-year job.
  • Broad ranking climb that can distract a specialist institution into wanting to look comprehensive.
  • City-state demand for people who can govern AI in finance, public service and firms.
  • Competition from NUS Business, NTU NBS, INSEAD Asia, and industry academies.
“We should be turning our attention to helping young people prepare themselves for what I call the third-year job.” — Lily Kong, ATxSummit 2026

Crux

SMU’s scarce problem is not recognition. It is whether a seminar university can graduate people who start at the work AI has not yet eaten.

Pedagogy holds · job redesigned

The Decision School

2030

A partner at a firm can tell you what an SMU seminar actually does. Students defend a live file — a deal, a case, a product call — with the internship supervisor in the room. Seminar size in the core is published and held. Starting salaries stay tight because the interview is short: they have already seen the work.

2040

SMU is the place the city sends people who will have to decide in public. The campus is still small. That is the product. Computing did not become a second NUS School of Computing on a smaller plot; law stayed a judgement studio.

Pedagogy holds · job flattened

Excellent Unemployed

2030

Teaching is still good. The third-year job market has thinned; too many students are writing essays about work they did not do. SMU-X is an extra rather than a graded requirement with an employer-side mark.

2040

A polished downtown degree whose holders are competing with cheaper online credentials for the same analyst seats. Pedagogy without a job design is a finishing school.

Content school · job redesigned

Downtown Utility

2030

Evening and modular programmes pay the bills. The undergraduate seminar shrinks to a boutique. Class sizes creep in the programmes that used to be the point. Downtown is convenient.

2040

SMU is a convenient city campus for stackable courses. The original bet — small room, city job — is a prospectus paragraph.

Content school · job flattened

Ranked and Hollow

2030

Rank and city address do the selling. Class sizes creep up. Internships become a career-office volume game. A student can graduate without once speaking every week in a room of forty.

2040

A well-ranked brand on Victoria Street with no method a student can point to. Employers run their own afternoon test and skip the transcript.

Implications

Do not spend SMU2030 on looking more like NUS. Spend it on being the university that can examine judgement in public. Kong’s diagnosis is already the strategy. Execution is assessment and hiring.

A bad strategy would be: add an analytics school, keep detection as the honour story, and let seminar size drift up until SMU is a lecture hall with a city postcode.

For students · SMU

The first analyst job is shrinking. Build the job that comes after it.

SMU is the small downtown university: seminar rooms, law and business, a computing school added later, internships in the city. President Lily Kong’s point at ATxSummit 2026 was blunt — agentic AI is eating the tasks that used to justify hiring a fresh graduate. The small room only helps you if you use it, and if the internship is real work a supervisor will sit with, not a line on a CV.

Best case for you

Decision School

A partner at a firm can tell you what an SMU seminar actually does. You defend a live file — a deal, a case, a product call — with the internship supervisor in the room. You are hired to run the work AI made faster, not to do the first-year analyst tasks it already does.

Comfortable trap

Excellent Unemployed

Teaching is still good. The junior market has thinned. You can think in a seminar and still wait six months for work that used to be the “first job.” Pedagogy without a designed third-year job is a finishing school.

Fine, replaceable

Downtown Utility

Evening and modular programmes pay the bills. Undergraduate seminars get larger. You leave with a solid city business degree you could have got elsewhere.

Worst case

Ranked and Hollow

Ranks and the Victoria Street address do the selling. Class sizes creep up. Internships become a career-office volume game. Soft skills are a slogan. You cannot point to a method.

Do this while you are here

  • Pick the seminar that makes you speak every week. Treat it as an oral defence — because that is how the useful versions of this degree will be examined.
  • In writing and reasoning modules, grade the model’s answer. Do not submit it as yours. The interview will do the same thing to you.
  • Use ResWORK, SMU-X and the internship as a second transcript. Get a named supervisor who will sit in your capstone. If you cannot explain one decision you made at work, the degree is not doing its job.
  • Do not hide in extra certificates. Stay in the city: the campus is the classroom only if you leave the building.
