Board two-pager

For the President and the Board of Trustees

Crux

Larry Jameson leaves on 30 June 2027. Penn Forward has nine initiatives and no owner who will still be in the chair when they bite. The hard problem is to appoint, before he goes, a president who will run Penn as one machine — hospital, Wharton, the rest of the schools — rather than a holding company of famous brands, and to freeze enough of Penn Forward that the search does not become a two-year pause.

Diagnosis in six lines

Penn’s comparative advantage is not any single school. It is the short walk between Wharton, Penn Medicine, Engineering, and a dense undergraduate college in a city it actually occupies. That advantage is easy to praise and hard to govern. Jameson was hired to stop the bleeding after Magill. He did. Penn Forward converted a vision document into working groups and then into nine initiatives. He has now dated his own exit. The Board is running a search in a sector where Washington is still writing conditions onto federal money, where an 8 percent endowment tax hits the same peer set, and where Wharton’s gravity can quietly set the university’s problem list if no one in College Hall is strong enough to say no.

Guiding policy

Integrate on purpose. The next president is hired to run the joints — AI across schools, clinical-research translation, undergraduate access — not to be a super-dean of the loudest school. Lock two or three Penn Forward bets as multi-year budget lines before the handover so they are not campaign promises.

Coherent actions

  1. Write the presidential spec as an integration job: published authority over joint hires, shared AI infrastructure, and the Medicine–Engineering–Wharton interfaces.
  2. Convert two Penn Forward initiatives (AI infrastructure; tuition/aid predictability) into Board-voted multi-year funding that survives a new president.
  3. Treat the Bay Area presence as a Wharton-and-engineering outpost with a cap, not as a second university that will demand its own faculty senate.
  4. Keep the compact-rejection position as policy: merit funding, no enrollment-mix bargain — and pair it with a non-federal research backfill so the next president is not forced to reopen the deal.
  5. Put West Philadelphia and the health system on the same civic ledger as the academic brands; a president who only manages Washington and Davos will lose the city that makes Penn distinctive.

Full report

What Penn is

A private urban university whose schools would each be a destination and whose bet, since the late twentieth century, has been that they are more valuable together. Wharton gives it a global professional network. Penn Medicine gives it a clinical-research engine and a civic footprint. The undergraduate college gives it a residential claim the professional schools cannot. Engineering is the hinge that either joins those pieces or sits beside them.

Forces that actually move this institution

  • A dated presidency. Jameson announced 12 June 2026 that he will finish on 30 June 2027. The search opened over the summer. He was always a stabilization president. Stabilization without a successor spec is how institutions drift back to school-level politics.
  • Penn Forward as a pile of good projects. Six working groups; nine initiatives in the April 2026 rollout — tuition and aid redesign, AI leadership, global/Bay Area reach, operations, student experience. Projects are not a kernel. They become a kernel only if two or three of them are funded past the handover.
  • Washington weather shared with the Ivies. Penn rejected the compact. It also entered a Title IX resolution that restored some flow. That combination — refuse the ideological bargain, settle the compliance bargain — will be the template the next president inherits. It is fragile.
  • Wharton gravity. The San Francisco campus and any deeper Bay Area build-out can pull problem choice toward markets and away from the hospital and the neighborhood. That is a feature if governed. It is capture if not.
  • City and health system. Penn is not a chapel in a meadow. Labor, patients, local schools, and real estate are part of the production system. A foresight pack that mentions only AI and the Ivy peer set has already misunderstood the place.

Use the last year of a stabilization presidency to lock integration as the job description — so the next president is not a referee among brands.

Kernel Reconstruction, 20 September 2026 — not Penn Forward

Diagnosis

Penn’s schools are strong enough to survive a weak center. That is the risk. After Magill, the center was rebuilt as calm. Calm is not integration. The search will default to another calm manager unless the Board writes integration into the warrant.

Guiding policy

Hire for the joints. Pre-commit money to two Forward bets. Cap the Bay Area as an outpost. Keep the compact refusal solvent. Count the city as part of the enterprise.

Coherent actions

An integration-first presidential spec. Board-voted multi-year funding for AI infrastructure and aid predictability. A cap on Bay Area institutionalization. A non-federal research backfill. A civic ledger that includes Medicine and West Philadelphia.

Critical uncertainties

Axis A — Federal bargain with the Ivies. Merit funding and international doctoral flow stabilize, or conditionality and tax remain the operating environment.

Axis B — Where power sits inside Penn. College Hall can move joint work, or the schools (especially Wharton and Medicine) resume being a confederacy.

Usable federal · Integrated

One Penn

2030

A new president has made joint hires that sit in two schools at once — Medicine and Engineering, Wharton and SAS — and those people have students. AI infrastructure is a shared utility, not a Wharton toy and a SEAS toy. Undergraduate aid is predictable enough that a family can plan. The Bay Area presence is an outpost of Philadelphia, not a second university that ignores College Hall.

2040

Penn is the urban research university others try to copy and cannot, because they do not own a hospital, a business school, and a neighborhood at the same time. A student can walk from a clinic to a studio to a trading floor without changing cities. That walk is the product. Other universities can rent one of the three. They cannot govern all three as one.

Conditional federal · Integrated

Civic Engine

2030

Federal money is awkward — slower, conditional, politically noisy. Integration still works because the health system and philanthropy carry discovery through the trough. West Philadelphia is not a backdrop; it is a load-bearing wall: clinics, schools, hiring. Undergraduates who never leave the Wharton quad miss the university that is actually paying the bills.

2040

Penn is less global and more grounded. Distinctive. Harder to rank. The people who stay know the hospital and the neighborhood. The people who wanted a frictionless global brand go to a peer that still sells one. Rankings twitch. The work in the clinics does not.

Usable federal · Confederacy

Brand Campus

2030

Money is fine. Each school optimizes itself: Wharton for Wharton, Medicine for Medicine, SEAS for SEAS. Undergraduates experience Penn as a subway map of excellent colleges that share a dining plan. Crossing the street is possible and rare. Joint degrees exist on paper. Advisors talk students out of them because the home school wants the headcount.

2040

Wharton and Medicine are global. The rest of Penn is a logo they share. A graduate can spend four years inside one school and tell the truth when they say they went to Penn. The integrated machine of the Gutmann years is a story older faculty tell.

Conditional federal · Confederacy

Holding Company

2030

Each school cuts its own deal with sponsors and with Washington. College Hall manages lawsuits, campaigns, and the search for the next president. A joint hire dies in two committees. Students who want a clinic-plus-studio find that neither side will own the risk.

2040

Penn is a famous collection. The integrated machine is a memory of the Gutmann years. Reunion tours walk a campus that still looks like one university. The org chart and the budget do not.

Implications

The search is the strategy. If the Board hires a consensus diplomat, Penn Forward will become a list of offices. If it hires someone who can move a joint hire against a dean’s preference, the rest of the pack is executable. Jameson’s last year is useful only if two budget lines outlive him.

For students · Penn

The useful Penn is the one between schools, not the one inside a single brand.

You will be here for the end of one presidency and the start of another. Cross the street more often than the org chart suggests.

Best case

One Penn

Take the joint degree, the clinic-plus-studio, the Wharton-and-SEAS project — the combination that only works if the center still holds. That combination is the scarce Penn credential. A single-school transcript is what every peer already sells.

Tighter federal

Civic Engine

Medicine and the city will carry more of the work. Learn the hospital and the neighborhood, not only the alumni network. If you never leave the Wharton quad, you will miss the university that is actually paying for this world.

School silos

Brand Campus

Your home school will take care of you. The rest of Penn will not, unless you walk there and make someone put their name on your project. Do that in year one, before the schools finish optimizing themselves.

Worst case

Holding Company

Collect a brand and a portable body of work. Do not assume College Hall will hold a door open between schools. If a project needs two signatures, get them on paper this term.

Do this while you are here

  • Do one piece of work that requires two schools to sign it.
  • If you are in Wharton, take a hospital or a lab seriously. If you are in Medicine or SEAS, learn how a market actually ships.
  • Use the city as a site, not a backdrop.
  • Watch the presidential search the way you would watch a product launch. It will set your last two years.
