Board two-pager

For the President and Board of Regents

Crux

James Milliken speaks for ten campuses and more than 300,000 students. Sacramento pays for seats. Washington pays for science when it chooses to. Berkeley and UCLA take the national heat. The hard problem is to remain one public research university — Merced and Riverside still able to make knowledge, not only to teach — without selling the research engine for a quieter budget year, and without letting the flagships become a private-feeling pair inside a public logo.

Diagnosis in six lines

Fall enrollment crossed 301,000. California resident undergraduates are on a path to 217,000 by 2030 under the Newsom compact. For fall 2026, Berkeley admitted 10.5 percent of first-year applicants and UCLA 10.8. The 2026–27 state package includes the $254 million Compact installment; it is not a replacement for federal research. Milliken told the Assembly that losing federal research would require $4–5 billion a year from the state to sustain reduced operations. CSU bought systemwide ChatGPT. UC has not. It is shopping among a frontier-model contract, a public cloud, and a UC-owned AI cloud, while campuses launch uneven degrees. The political targeting of Berkeley and UCLA is a system-architecture problem: heat at the flagships, enrollment mandates everywhere else, science paid from a different treasury.

Guiding policy

One university, two treasuries, explicit rules. Sacramento buys access and California talent. Federal and philanthropic money buy discovery. Flagships are not punished for being visible, and inland campuses are not parked as overflow. AI is a system capability, not ten shopping trips.

Coherent actions

  1. Put a number on the federal-research hole at each campus and take that number to Sacramento as a science appropriation, separate from the Compact seat-price.
  2. Choose an AI path in 2027 — system contract, public cloud, or UC cloud — and stop the current vendor shrug.
  3. Write a flagship-protection doctrine: legal defense, research continuity, and communications are system duties when Berkeley or UCLA is the designated target.
  4. Fund AI-for-the-physical-economy and public-service work at Davis, San Diego, Irvine, Riverside, Santa Cruz, and Merced so “research university” is not a synonym for two coastal brands.

Start

A number on the federal-research hole at each campus, taken to Sacramento as science money. An AI path chosen in 2027. A flagship-protection doctrine when Berkeley or UCLA is the target.

Stop

Vendor shrug. Treating ‘research university’ as a synonym for two logos. Compact seat-price as if it were a science policy.

Go

The transfer function. Multi-campus public science. Fund Davis, San Diego, Irvine, Riverside, Santa Cruz, Merced as research universities — adapt the budget, do not abandon the system.

Full report

What the UC system is

A constitutional public research university of ten campuses, five medical centers, and three national labs. Berkeley and UCLA are the national symbols. Davis, San Diego, Irvine, Santa Barbara, Santa Cruz, Riverside, Merced, and UCSF are the rest of the actual university. Treating the symbols as the system is how the system dies.

Forces that actually move this institution

  • The Compact bargain. State money is tied to California enrollment growth. That is a legitimate democratic demand. It is also how a research university is quietly re-specified as an access utility.
  • Federal politicization. Berkeley and UCLA have been the face of administration pressure, including a 2025 UCLA grant freeze near $600 million and a $1.2 billion settlement demand later blocked in court. Science funding can also be slowed without a single dramatic freeze.
  • Flagship congestion. Admit rates near 10 percent at Berkeley and UCLA coexist with Merced near-open admission among eligible Californians. One name, two products.
  • AI incoherence. CSU made a loud system bet. UC is still designing the bet. Campuses are already making local ones.
“If UC were to lose federal research funds, the University would need $4 billion to $5 billion annually to sustain reduced core operations and research.” — James B. Milliken, February 2026

Crux

Remain one public research university — access and discovery, flagships and the rest — when the political incentives are to grow seats, starve contested campuses, and let the mid-system drift into teaching-only.

Kernel Reconstruction, September 2026 — not the published plan

Diagnosis

UC is winning the enrollment argument and losing the architecture argument. Record California numbers please Sacramento. They do not replace NIH, NSF, or DOE.

Guiding policy

Name the two treasuries and refuse to let either one pretend to be the other. Protect the flagships as system assets. Build research identity at the other campuses around California’s physical and public problems. Choose an AI architecture as a system.

Coherent actions

Campus-level federal-research stress table, public to the Regents. A 2027 AI architecture decision with a student-access floor so Merced does not wait out Berkeley’s vendors. Flagship defense as a standing UCOP function. Directed research missions for Davis, San Diego, Irvine, Riverside, Santa Cruz, Merced — not mini-Berkeleys.

Critical uncertainties

Axis A — Science treasury. Federal science remains a workable national bargain, or UC is forced toward a state-funded teaching system with boutique research islands.

Axis B — Systemhood. Ten campuses plus UCSF operate as one research enterprise, or flagships, medical centers, and inland campuses take separate paths.

Science holds · One UC

The Public Stack

2030

Compact seats are actually funded — a student at Merced can find the class, not only the promise. California has put a science backstop under federal shocks, so a lab at Davis or San Diego does not die on a Washington timetable. There is a system path for AI infrastructure that more than two campuses can use. Berkeley and UCLA are still loud, still public, and still sending students to the other eight.

2040

UC is the world’s most serious public research system, and discovery is no longer a two-campus story. A field can start at Irvine or Santa Cruz and finish with a Berkeley colleague. The Compact bought access. The system spent the decade buying research capacity to match. A UC degree means something common across ten seals.

Teaching system · One UC

The People’s Utility

2030

Enrollment targets are hit. Federal science has shrunk and California did not replace it at research scale. The system stays together by becoming a very large teaching university with a few remaining labs at the flagships. A student at Riverside can get a seat. They cannot get a bench. Transfer still works. Discovery concentrates.

2040

California got access. It no longer has a public institution that can set a scientific field from the middle of the system. The flagships look like well-endowed islands. The other campuses look like a state college system that kept the UC name. Families got seats. The state lost a research engine it will notice ten years later.

Science holds · Separate

Two UCs

2030

Berkeley, UCLA, San Diego and UCSF keep science — grants, hospitals, graduate programs. The other campuses become regional access engines with a research story in the viewbook. A student who wanted “UC research” without a flagship admit finds the difference in year two, when the lab they wanted is a four-hour drive and not taking visitors.

2040

There is a de facto split: Oxbridge-on-the-Pacific plus a state college system, without anyone voting for it. UCOP still prints one logo. Employers have already learned which four campuses count. Inland and newer campuses do the access work and do not share the discovery work.

Teaching system · Separate

The Breakup

2030

Flagships cut their own deals with Washington and with donors. Medical centers ring-fence clinical revenue. Inland campuses live on the Compact and on teaching load. A systemwide AI or climate initiative dies in the first budget pass because no campus will put its surplus into another campus’s lab.

2040

“University of California” is a holding company over institutions that no longer share a fate or a standard. A degree from Merced and a degree from Berkeley share a seal and not much else. Students who moved between campuses find that credits transfer more easily than opportunity does.

Implications

The bad strategy is to celebrate record enrollment and treat Berkeley/UCLA politics as campus-level noise. Across worlds, the robust moves are: price science separately from seats, decide AI as a system, and refuse a silent split between flagships and the rest.

For students · UC

You did not apply to a logo. You applied to one campus inside a system that may or may not stay one university.

If you are at Berkeley or UCLA, the national fight will feel like your weather. If you are at Merced or Riverside, the Compact will feel like your weather. Both are the same story: what a public research university is for.

Best case

The Public Stack

Your campus can still do research, not only teach. The system actually shares tools — compute, data, a path off your campus if the lab is elsewhere. A UC degree means something common. Use the other campuses while that is true.

Access without science

The People’s Utility

Classes will be full. Labs will not. Learn the skills that travel — a method, a language, a clinical or field practice — because the bench on your campus may not be there when you are ready for it.

Split

Two UCs

The name is shared. The opportunity is not. Hunt the specific department and the specific faculty member, not the system brand. If you are not at a flagship, plan the extra year or the extra campus move early.

Worst case

The Breakup

Governance noise will eat years. Build a portfolio that does not depend on UCOP holding the system together: a paper, a skill, a network on one campus that still functions if the logo war continues.

Do this while you are here

  • Go Use the system: another campus, a national lab, a medical center, a field station. That is the point of UC.
  • Start Learn an AI workflow and a method that does not need one.
  • Start If you are at a flagship, notice who is missing from the seminar because a grant died.
  • Start If you are not at a flagship, attach yourself to that campus’s distinctive mission rather than imitating College Ave.
  • Stop Stop assuming the Compact paid for your lab. Ask which campus fund actually did.
