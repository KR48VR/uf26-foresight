University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign · Scratch rebuild · 20 September 2026

Illinois already has the computing faculty. What it does not yet have is a second production system in Chicago that does not hollow out Urbana.

Charles Isbell’s “Illinois Leads” plan landed in September 2026. Discovery Partners Institute now reports through Urbana: Gene Robinson as CEO, Rashid Bashir as vice chancellor for Chicago partnerships, a purchased West Loop headquarters at 250 S. Wacker, IBM’s Discovery Accelerator renewed in April. The land-grant engine is still downstate. The political and industrial weather is in Chicago. That split is the strategy if it becomes one system. It is a leak if it becomes two campuses with one logo.

Board two-pager

For the Chancellor and the Board of Trustees

Crux

Illinois now owns a West Loop headquarters, an IBM quantum-and-AI accelerator, and a plan called Illinois Leads. Urbana still has the labs, the students, and the land-grant habit. The hard problem is to make Chicago a second place where work is finished — not a showroom that borrows Urbana faculty for the ribbon-cutting and then keeps them on the train until they stop coming home.

Diagnosis in six lines

UIUC is one of the few public universities that can still claim a global computer-science and engineering bench without coastal rents. That bench was built in Urbana. The state, the system, and the companies now want the same bench to be visible on the Chicago River. DPI’s reboot is the test: Robinson is a serious scientist; Bashir holds both Grainger and the Chicago brief; the West Loop building is real estate with a mandate. “Illinois Leads” is still a layered slogan until someone can say which dissertations, which startups, and which faculty lines live in which city — and what Urbana is no longer asked to pretend to be.

Guiding policy

Urbana remains the tenure home and the doctoral factory. Chicago is the deployment and partnership floor. Faculty may split time; lines may not silently migrate. Measure DPI by companies and public problems moved, not by how many Urbana stars have a second office.

Coherent actions

  1. Publish a two-city faculty compact: tenure home, teaching load, and Chicago-day expectations, with a cap on how many Urbana lines can be majority-Chicago.
  2. Staff DPI with a small number of Chicago-primary faculty (Bashir’s own planning number was on the order of 25) rather than an unbounded visiting rota from Grainger.
  3. Tie IBM, quantum-park, and state money to shared instruments that Urbana graduate students can book — not to a parallel lab they cannot enter.
  4. Make “Illinois Leads” Level 3 measurement include a single public dashboard: doctoral yield, industry-sponsored volume, and the Urbana/Chicago split of both.
  5. Keep the land-grant rest of the campus (agriculture, education, the arts) in the story, or Chicago-computing will eat the university’s political license downstate.

Start

A two-city faculty compact: tenure home, teaching load, Chicago-day cap. Staff DPI with a small Chicago-primary faculty, not an unbounded Urbana rota. Tie IBM and quantum-park money to instruments Urbana graduate students can book.

Stop

Silent migration of lines to Chicago. Measuring DPI by how many Urbana stars have a second office. A parallel lab graduate students cannot enter.

Go

Urbana remains the tenure home and the doctoral factory. Chicago is the deployment floor. Faculty may split time; lines may not silently migrate.

Full report

What UIUC is

A full land-grant university whose global reputation is disproportionately carried by computing, engineering, and a few adjacent sciences, set in a small twin city two hours from the market that now wants to own the story. Isbell, a computing researcher himself, is the first chancellor in a generation for whom the Chicago move is not a side quest.

Forces that actually move this institution

  • DPI as a second campus in all but name. Oversight moved to the Urbana chancellor. Robinson named CEO January 2026. Headquarters purchased at 250 S. Wacker (245,000 sq ft) in February 2026. Focus restated as AI, quantum, future computing.
  • Industrial policy with a state face. IBM–Illinois Discovery Accelerator renewed and expanded 16 April 2026, with sites in Urbana and at DPI. Quantum Works and related park activity sit next to Pritzker’s Midwest computing bet.
  • A new plan without a new constraint. “Illinois Leads,” announced mid-September 2026, has three layers of goals. Layer 1 is broad enough to bless anything. The useful part is Layer 3 — measurement — if anyone puts Chicago/Urbana split on the dashboard.
  • System politics. President Killeen still speaks for three universities. UIC will not love a story in which Urbana owns Chicago tech. The compact has to be written as a system asset that Urbana operates, not a raid.
  • Federal and talent weather shared with every US peer. Visa caps, agency conditionality, and industry hiring of doctoral talent hit Urbana harder than they hit Stanford, because the compensating local industry is two hours away.
“We think the focus should be the future of computing, and the future of computing is AI and quantum. To advance quantum you need advances in AI.” — Rashid Bashir, on the DPI reboot

Make Chicago a multiplier of Urbana’s doctoral engine rather than an alternative employer of the same fifty people.

Kernel Reconstruction, 20 September 2026 — not the published plan

Diagnosis

The university is trying to be a coastal computing power without coastal geography. That only works if Chicago is a dock, not a drain. Right now the org chart says dock and the incentives say drain.

Guiding policy

Tenure and dissertations stay downstate. Partnerships and deployment sit in the West Loop. Count both, in public, as one system.

Coherent actions

Two-city faculty compact with a migration cap. A small Chicago-primary faculty, not an unbounded rota. Shared instruments that Urbana students can book. A dashboard that splits doctoral and sponsor activity by city. Protect the non-computing land-grant so the political license holds.

Critical uncertainties

Axis A — Midwest industrial policy. State, IBM, and federal place-based computing money stays real through 2030, or it proves to be a cycle.

Axis B — Where the faculty actually live. Urbana remains the gravity well, or lines and students drift to Chicago and to industry.

Policy holds · Urbana gravity

Two-City Engine

2030

A graduate student can book IBM time from a Urbana lab on Monday and sit with a Chicago partner on Wednesday without changing advisors. DPI’s West Loop building is busy after 6 p.m., which is the test that it is not only a tour. Illinois Leads has a public dashboard — seats, internships, joint appointments — that departments actually use. The land-grant engine did not move. It grew a front door.

2040

Illinois is a public computing power that did not have to move to the coast. A company that wants talent still comes to Urbana for the bench and to the West Loop for the deployment. Chicago is the front door. Urbana is still the house. A degree from either end of the train is readable as one university.

Policy fades · Urbana gravity

Downstate Core

2030

The West Loop building photographs well and is quiet on a Thursday. Grainger, NCSA and the Urbana departments keep quality the old way: grants, students, machines. Springfield still asks why the Chicago story was sold. Faculty who tried the two-city life come home and stay. Students notice that the “Chicago opportunity” in the brochure is a visitor badge and a packed lunch.

2040

UIUC is still a great public research university that tried Chicago and came home. That is not a failure. It is not the bet that was sold to the Board and the Governor. The West Loop asset becomes a liaison office. The interesting work never needed the train.

Policy holds · Drift

River Campus

2030

Money and headlines sit in Chicago. The new hires live there too. Urbana’s undergraduate brand still holds — parents still send eighteen-year-olds to the Quad — but the faculty who would have been their research mentors take the train north on Sunday night. A senior thesis advisor is harder to find than a DPI tour.

2040

UIUC is a Chicago tech institute with a large downstate college attached. Employers learn two brands and pick the Loop one. Urbana becomes the place you start, not the place you finish. The land-grant sentence stays on the website.

Policy fades · Drift

Split Brand

2030

DPI stalls — delayed tenants, a political fight, a CEO transition. Urbana loses people to industry anyway, because the talent market does not wait for a headquarters to work. Champaign and Chicago argue about who failed whom. Students hear two stories and trust neither.

2040

What remains is a weaker land-grant and a West Loop building looking for an owner. Rankings slip just enough to notice. The IBM relationship becomes a logo on a door. A student who wanted both cities gets neither at full strength.

Implications

Isbell’s plan will be judged on whether the next twenty computing hires have Urbana kitchens. Everything else is communications. The Board should not accept a Chicago success metric that is silent on Urbana occupancy.

For students · UIUC

Urbana is still where the degree is forged. Chicago is where it gets used. You need both on purpose, not by accident.

The next four years will decide whether DPI is a door you can walk through or a rumor two hours north.

Best case

Two-City Engine

Do the thesis in Urbana, where the machine and the advisor live, and a deployment term in the West Loop, where the partner lives. Write both on the same document. That combination — not either city alone — is the scarce Illinois product in 2026.

Plan B works

Downstate Core

Chicago may cool. The Urbana labs will not. Stay close to NCSA, Grainger and one professor who actually answers mail. Treat the brochure’s Chicago page as optional.

Gravity shift

River Campus

The interesting work may move north. Get on the train early enough to be known in the West Loop. Keep a downstate advisor who can still sign a thesis if the Chicago project evaporates.

Worst case

Split Brand

Neither city will be sure it owns you. Build a portable body of work — code, a paper, a deployed prototype — and one network that does not depend on Urbana or the Loop still talking to each other.

Do this while you are here

  • Start Take one Chicago-facing project (DPI, IBM, a firm) and one Urbana-only problem that would exist without Chicago.
  • Start Learn the instrument stack at NCSA well enough that a West Loop office cannot replace it.
  • Start If you are not in computing, demand that “Illinois Leads” still has a sentence about your field — or leave with a second skill.
  • Start Treat the two-hour trip as part of the curriculum, not a weekend.
  • Stop Stop commuting to Chicago as if that were a second degree. Know which city owns your time.
