Board two-pager

For the President and the Corporation

Crux

Harvard spent 2025–26 in a fight with Washington: refused a set of federal demands, sued, and won the opening rounds. The endowment is enormous. NIH awards and visas are not. The hard problem is to come through that fight still a research university — labs that can hire, hospitals that can trial, graduate students who can get a visa — rather than a smaller, richer, more American college that kept the brand and lost the science.

Diagnosis in six lines

In April 2025 the administration froze about $2.2 billion and terminated a reported 950 awards. Harvard sued. Courts restored much of the money and blocked the attempt to kill visa-hosting. A further lawsuit seeking to rescind federal funding was dismissed in August 2026. That is the first war. The second is quieter: NIH awards through FY2026 still run about 18 percent behind the Biden-era pace; the endowment tax rises to 8 percent and is estimated at $200–300 million a year from 2027; combined federal-plus-tax pressure has been put as high as $1 billion a year. Foreign gifts still arrive — $163 million year-to-date 2026 — and attract their own scrutiny. The endowment grew to $56.9 billion in FY2025. A growing endowment over a thinning lab is the failure mode.

Guiding policy

Spend wealth to keep the scientific and international university intact, not to wait out politics in comfort. The brand can survive a decade of hostility. The wet labs, the hospitals, and the graduate pipeline cannot be rebuilt later at any price.

Coherent actions

  1. Extend Research Continuity Funding past the current reserve window and treat it as a multi-year bridge, not an emergency check.
  2. Ring-fence wet-lab and hospital science so the endowment tax is not paid out of NIH-substituting activity.
  3. Build a legally boring, operationally excellent international-student machine that does not depend on one court date.
  4. Do not let undergraduate politics become the whole presidency. The scientific university lives in the schools and the hospitals.
  5. Publish a simple annual dashboard: new federal awards, graduate yield by citizenship, PI departures, hospital trial volume.

Start

Extend Research Continuity Funding as a multi-year bridge, not an emergency check. Ring-fence wet-lab and hospital science from the endowment tax. A legally boring international-student machine that does not depend on one court date.

Stop

Waiting out politics in comfort. Paying the endowment tax out of NIH-substituting activity. Letting undergraduate politics become the whole presidency.

Go

Spend wealth to keep the scientific and international university intact. The brand can survive hostility. The wet labs, hospitals and graduate pipeline cannot be rebuilt later.

Full report

What Harvard is

The oldest American university, a decentralized federation of faculties, and the owner of a medical-scientific complex that is larger than most universities. Alan Garber, a physician-economist, chose litigation over a political settlement in 2025. That choice is now the strategy whether the Corporation likes it or not.

Forces that actually move this institution

  • Weaponized federal science. Grant freezes and award terminations were used as governance tools. Courts blocked the blunt instruments. Agencies can still starve by pace.
  • Endowment tax as industrial policy. The 8 percent tier is a recurring cash levy on the private research model.
  • International-student chokepoint. The attempt to revoke visa-hosting was stayed. The appeal is live. The American research university is being told to nationalize its classroom.
  • Decentralized wealth. Schools and hospitals do not share one fate. FAS science and HMS can shrink while the undergraduate college remains oversubscribed.
  • Political salience. Harvard is the cheapest place to stage a fight with elite higher education.
Harvard held. It sued. It won. The uncertainties have not dissolved; they have merely shifted form.

Crux

Keep the research-hospital-international university alive inside a brand that can survive almost anything — including a decade in which Washington treats Harvard as an opponent rather than an instrument.

Kernel Reconstruction, September 2026 — not the published plan

Diagnosis

The 2025 confrontation was existential in rhetoric and partial in cash. The 2026 condition is more dangerous because it looks survivable. An 8 percent tax plus a slower NIH plus a risk-off graduate office will, over five years, produce a university that is still Harvard in the guidebooks and no longer Harvard in the labs.

Guiding policy

Defend the right to be a global scientific university in court, and fund that university as if the court might lose. Do not trade the graduate pipeline or the hospitals for a quieter undergraduate year.

Coherent actions

Multi-year research bridge with sunset criteria tied to award-pace recovery. Internal tax-incidence rule: the 8 percent levy is not paid by shrinking wet science. International graduate compact robust to a lost appeal. Corporation dashboard that makes “richer but smaller” visible. Quiet coalition with other taxed institutions on the levy, separate from the culture-war docket.

Critical uncertainties

Axis A — Federal science bargain. Courts and agencies restore a workable grant state, or a durable politicized regime treats Harvard as a designated opponent.

Axis B — Composition of the university. Global research-and-hospital Harvard holds, or a wealthier, more domestic, more undergraduate Harvard with a thinner scientific core.

Restored · Global

The Republic’s Lab

2030

NIH and other awards are close to the old pace. A first-year PhD from overseas can still get here in time for term. Continuity funding that plugged 2025 holes is being retired on purpose, not because the labs vanished. A principal investigator spends the week on experiments and students, not on a second full-time job of emergency fundraising. Undergraduates still sit next to international graduates in the lab.

2040

Harvard is again treated as national infrastructure: the hospital trials, the labs, the graduate pipeline. The 2025–27 years read as an episode in the history, not as the new operating system. A twenty-year-old arriving in 2040 is told the story the way an earlier generation was told about the 1970s. The science is still made here.

Starvation · Global

Endowed Resistance

2030

Federal science stays hostile — slower awards, more conditions, visa friction. Harvard spends endowment income to keep principal investigators and international graduates on payroll. Labs run. They run smaller. A faculty meeting includes a standing item on which offers to match and which countries still issue the documents. The College is fine. The discovery engine is a privately financed island inside a public fight.

2040

Harvard is a privately financed scientific island. Excellent, smaller, and permanently at war with parts of the American state. The people who stay do serious work. The people who would have come from overseas go to Toronto, London or Singapore. Reunion speeches call this independence. The citation charts call it a narrowing.

Restored · Smaller

Quiet Restoration

2030

The headlines calm down. Grants return. Harvard has already resized graduate science and become more domestic: fewer slots, fewer international admits, a quieter medical research enterprise. Peace arrives after the capacity has left. A lab that used to run two postdocs and a cluster of graduates now runs one of each and calls it focus.

2040

Harvard is a beautiful college with professional schools and a medical brand. The discovery engine is elsewhere — Boston’s other hospitals, the West Coast, a national lab. Applicants still come for the name. Doctoral students who want to make a field go where the grants and the group already are.

Starvation · Smaller

The Museum

2030

Applicants still come. Labs close by non-replacement: a retirement is not a search. International graduates choose other countries because the visa and the welcome both failed. Undergraduates experience a rich College and a thinner research university and may not notice the difference until they apply for a PhD somewhere else.

2040

Harvard is still the most famous American university. It is no longer one of the ten places where the scientific frontier is made. The Yard photographs the same. The papers that used to start here start in other cities. The Museum is a working description, not an insult from outside.

Implications

The bad strategy is to treat litigation wins as strategy and endowment growth as health. A policy that only works in The Republic’s Lab fails in the other three worlds. The robust policy is to fund the scientific and international core as if the political weather will not break.

For students · Harvard

The fight is about whether this place still makes new knowledge, not whether it still gets into the news.

You arrived at a university that spent a year in federal court. That is not a campus aesthetic. It is a warning about which parts of Harvard are durable and which parts can be starved.

Best case

The Republic’s Lab

Grants return. Your supervisor is in the lab, not on a second shift of emergency fundraising. International classmates arrive and stay. If you want research, this is the world in which Harvard still behaves like a research university.

Hard virtue

Endowed Resistance

The work continues on Harvard’s own dime. Expect fewer research slots, more political weather than you asked for, and a classmate mix that is more domestic than the brochure implied. Stay close to a lab that still has people in it.

Soft loss

Quiet Restoration

The headlines will calm down after the labs have already thinned. Do not confuse a quieter Washington with a working bench. Ask how many graduates your group actually has before you commit a thesis to it.

Worst case

The Museum

The name will still open doors. The discovery will happen in other cities. Build skills and a paper trail that travel without the letterhead — a method, a dataset, a clinical result — before you need them.

Do this while you are here

  • Go Attach yourself to a lab, archive, clinic, or field site that produces evidence, not takes.
  • Start Learn how a grant actually runs. That is civic education in 2026.
  • Start Have at least one serious collaboration with someone who is not on a US passport.
  • Start If you are in the College, walk to Longwood or the SEAS labs before you decide what Harvard is.
  • Stop Stop letting the undergraduate argument eat the scientific university you came for.
