Singapore Institute of Technology · Scratch rebuild · September 2026

SIT built a campus next to the firms. The crux is whether those firms will hire the competency — or keep hiring the older names and use Punggol as the view.

Punggol opened as a single home beside Punggol Digital District. Living labs, an eight-to-twelve-month work attachment, competency-based stackable degrees. UOB and OCBC have committed over S$1 billion to the district. Co-location is an option. It is not yet a labour market.

Board two-pager

For the President and Board of Trustees

Crux

Turn competency-based applied learning into a labour-market currency that does not need the NUS brand as a translator — using the Punggol living lab and the long work attachment as the proof, not as architecture for its own sake.

Diagnosis in six lines

SIT spent a decade getting out of six satellite sites and into Punggol. The campus is live: water, heritage trail, Punggol Coast MRT, JTC Business Park, 20,000 sensors, a multi-energy microgrid. Officials already cite high relevant-job rates. The next decade is different. NUS and NTU teach the same AI tools. If the competency transcript is real, SIT becomes the place firms use to hire. If it is a parallel paper, SIT remains the respectable third choice with a nicer campus. Status anxiety will push SIT toward looking more like NTU. That would waste Punggol.

Guiding policy

Make the Integrated Work Study Programme and the Living Lab the examined core. Partner with a small number of firms on assessments they will actually use in hiring. Do not chase research rankings that rewrite the faculty incentive away from applied work.

Coherent actions

  1. Publish an annual district ledger: which PDD firms took students, which problems were live, who hired.
  2. Keep IWSP at eight to twelve months. Shortening it to look more like a conventional university is how the model dies.
  3. Use the sensor campus as student work product that firms can see.
  4. Hold applied-research appointments to translational output. A second-rate imitation of NTU research is a worse SIT.
  5. Hire faculty with operator careers as a first-class track, and promote them.

Full report

What it is

SIT is Singapore’s university of applied learning, invented to take polytechnic talent up to degree level and to keep industry inside the curriculum. Punggol is the first time the institution has a single home next to the industries it claims.

Forces that actually move this institution

  • PDD filling with banks, public-tech, Panasonic — a once-only adjacency.
  • National push for skills-based hiring that employers praise and often ignore.
  • AI and cybersecurity demand that needs operators, not only researchers.
  • Status hierarchy that still ranks SIT below the two comprehensive universities.
“The only university campus in Singapore to be co-located with a business district.” — Lawrence Wong, Punggol opening

Crux

Adjacency without contracts is landscaping. SIT’s scarce problem is converting the district into a labour market that recognises applied work.

Kernel Reconstruction, September 2026 — not SIT’s published plan

Diagnosis

The building is done. The market is not. Status anxiety will push SIT toward looking more like NTU. That would waste Punggol.

Guiding policy

Contracts, work-study default, operator-faculty. Do not chase comprehensive prestige.

Coherent actions

District ledger. IWSP held at length. Campus as dataset. Applied-research appointments judged on translation. Operator-faculty track.

Firms hire · stays applied

District University

2030

A hiring manager in Punggol or Jurong can describe last year’s SIT capstone they used. Students spend a serious fraction of the degree inside the firm — the Integrated Work Study Programme is still eight to twelve months, not a ceremonial term. Partner firms are named on the degree page; partners who do not take students have been dropped. The sensor campus produces work product firms can see.

2040

SIT is how Singapore produces applied graduates without routing everyone through NTU. The district is the campus. A student who wanted the comprehensive look went elsewhere; a student who wanted to be hired off the work stayed.

Firms hire · imitates NTU

Punggol Tech

2030

The new campus photographs well. Industry language is everywhere. Actual work terms are shorter and more ceremonial. Research centres exist to look comprehensive. A capstone can be completed without a named person at a firm.

2040

A handsome applied-looking campus whose graduates still need an NTU or NUS master’s to be taken seriously. Punggol is a tour route.

Brand-hire · stays applied

Proud Outsider

2030

SIT leans into ‘we are not NTU’ but does not deepen the firm relationship. Identity is a chip on the shoulder. Work terms exist; conversion to jobs is uneven. The living lab is architecture for its own sake.

2040

A permanent understudy. Distinct, under-used. Employers still translate the degree through an NUS or NTU brand.

Brand-hire · imitates NTU

Campus Next Door

2030

SIT adds more conventional faculties and research talk. Status anxiety won. The work term is shortened to look more like a conventional university — which is how the model dies.

2040

A mid-tier comprehensive next to a new town, with neither applied muscle nor flagship prestige.

Implications

The Board should ask for names: which team at UOB, which GovTech product, which CSA problem. If the answer is “we are in discussions,” Punggol is still a construction story.

A bad strategy would be: shorten IWSP, start a rankings campaign, and call co-location a partnership.

For students · SIT

The firms are next door. Walk over.

SIT is the applied university next to the firms in Punggol. The degree title now says “university.” That only changes your life if a named company already knows your capstone or work-term before you graduate. If you spend year three trying to look like an NTU engineer on paper, you used the wrong campus.

Best case for you

District University

A hiring manager in Punggol or Jurong can describe last year’s SIT capstone they used. You spent a serious fraction of the degree inside the firm. Your living-lab work is already a hiring packet.

Looks applied, isn’t

Punggol Tech

The new campus photographs well. Work terms are shorter and more ceremonial. You get a job, but it is unclear why SIT was required rather than any other degree plus an internship.

Chip on the shoulder

Proud Outsider

You can do the work. HR still asks about NUS or NTU. SIT leaned into “we are not them” without deepening the firm relationship. Identity is a posture, not a method.

Worst case

Campus Next Door

Nice MRT, more conventional faculties, generic “innovation” talk. A mid-tier comprehensive next to a new town, with neither applied muscle nor flagship prestige.

Do this while you are here

  • Choose the work term like you choose a major. Keep a portfolio of things a firm shipped, not a list of modules.
  • Treat JTC Business Park as part of campus. Collect names. If a lecturer cannot name the partner on your degree, switch.
  • Use the campus sensors and microgrid as project data. Prefer work-study over a prettier transcript module.
  • Do not apologise for applied work. Apologise only if the work is generic.
