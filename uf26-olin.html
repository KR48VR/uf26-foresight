Board two-pager

For the President and Board of Trustees

Crux

Olin has about 350 students. Its entire claim is that engineering school can be rebuilt around making and impact, not around lectures. Agents can now do a disturbing amount of the junior project work that used to prove the method. The hard problem is to show, in the room, that a studio defense still mints judgment an agent cannot submit — not to stay small and precious, and not to become a museum of 2002 pedagogy with better furniture.

Diagnosis in six lines

Olin was founded to embarrass conventional engineering schools. Project from day one, integrated humanities, every student on an industry or impact capstone. The 2022–27 plan named that Impact-Centered Education. Teaching Fellows try to grow the teaching bench. Meanwhile a former Olin provost is helping build Greenway Institute — a new, cheaper, hands-on college that treats Olin as prior art. Agents now scaffold CAD, code, reports, and user-research writeups. Olin’s scarcity used to be the studio itself. The new scarcity has to be judgment in the studio.

Guiding policy

Be the open reference design, not the boutique. Publish the assessment. Let others copy. Charge for the thing that does not copy easily: faculty who can run a studio in which agents are tools and students are still responsible.

Coherent actions

  1. Redesign first- and second-year projects so an agent-finished artifact without a defended decision fails.
  2. Make Teaching Fellows a visible export product — train other campuses, do not only staff Olin.
  3. Publish an Olin Studio Protocol for AI: what is allowed, what must be witnessed, what must be orally defended.
  4. Partner with one large public engineering school as a scale test, or accept that influence will stall.
  5. Keep the college small; scale the method, not the headcount.

Full report

What Olin is

A very small undergraduate engineering college created as a demonstration. Its power was never enrollment. It was a proof that a different curriculum could exist. That proof is now twenty-plus years old and faces a tool that can impersonate the first two years of making.

Forces that actually move this institution

  • Agent-finished junior work. The studio’s early artifacts are newly cheap.
  • Copycats and successors. Greenway Institute and others treat Olin as a model to be cheapened and re-run.
  • Scale paradox. Growing Olin kills density. Not exporting the method kills relevance.
  • Teaching bench. The Fellows fund is an admission that the constraint is people who can teach this way.
“The future of engineering education depends on educators who are thoughtful, creative and deeply committed to their students.” — Sam Michalka, on the Teaching Fellows Fund

Crux

Prove the studio still produces judgment after agents can produce the artifact — and export that proof before someone else exports a thinner copy.

Kernel Reconstruction, September 2026 — not the published plan

Diagnosis

Olin’s founding enemy was lecture-and-problem-set engineering school. That enemy is still real and is no longer the main one. The main one is a world in which project-based education can be faked at high fluency.

Guiding policy

Judgment is the product. The method is open. Headcount stays small. Influence is measured in other people’s catalogs.

Coherent actions

Assessment redesign against agent-finished work. Published Studio Protocol. Teaching Fellows as export. One public-university scale partner. Refuse enrollment vanity.

Critical uncertainties

Axis A — What the studio certifies. Judgment remains scarce and examinable, or agents flatten the first two years into promptable making.

Axis B — How Olin matters. Open reference design used elsewhere, or a beloved boutique that cannot scale its influence.

Judgment · Reference

The Protocol

2030

Two or three larger schools are running Olin-like studios with Olin-trained fellows in the room. Agents are allowed in the studio and cannot pass the defense alone: a student has to stand up, point at a decision, and survive a faculty member who has seen the prompted version. Visiting deans come to Needham to watch a Friday afternoon, not to photograph a space.

2040

Olin is still small, and it is cited the way a standard is cited. A curriculum committee in another country will say “the Olin defense” and mean a specific ritual: impact brief, artifact, live argument, fail allowed. The 350-person college did not grow. It shipped a protocol.

Flattened · Reference

Pretty Template

2030

The method spreads and thins. Everyone has a studio and a wall of artifacts. Few have a defense that can fail a student. Agents fill the junior work. Olin’s name is on the slide that launched a hundred copies. The copies do not include the part that hurts.

2040

Olin’s name is on a pedagogy that no longer works. Employers have learned that “Olin-style studio” on a transcript means a handsome project and an untested decision. The original campus is still sincere. The protocol, once copied without the fail, is a style.

Judgment · Boutique

Needham Monastery

2030

Olin itself is superb. Visiting teams say they cannot run it at their scale and go home. Influence plateaus. The college stays excellent for 350 people and becomes a pilgrimage site rather than a reference design. Students get a rare education that does not travel as a method.

2040

Olin is a perfect college that history moved around. The people who went through it are devoted. The field of engineering education cites other experiments now. Needham remains a good place to become an engineer. It is no longer a place that changes other places.

Flattened · Boutique

Exhibit

2030

Projects look great on the wall. Defenses do not bite. Copies at other schools look the same because they copied the wall, not the argument. Agents produce most of the junior artifacts. Faculty praise process. Nobody can say what a student decided.

2040

Olin is a campus tour: a beautiful shop, a story about 2002, a wall of objects. Visitors take pictures. Students graduate with artifacts and a thin trail of decisions. The refusal that made Olin — that a project is not done until someone can fail it — is no longer in the room.

Implications

The bad strategy is to protect uniqueness by not publishing the method. That only works in a world without copycats and agents. The robust move is to make judgment examinable and the protocol public.

For students · Olin

If an agent can finish your project, the project was not the education.

Olin only works if you use the studio to practice decisions you will have to defend when the tool is better than it is this term.

Best case

The Protocol

You will learn a way of working other campuses will try to copy. Your defense is the product, not the object on the wall. Treat every Friday as if an outside examiner might fail you. That is the scarce skill.

Dilution

Pretty Template

Everyone will claim your method. Keep the judgment piece for yourself: the memo that says what you would not build, the test that could have sunk the project, the hour you did without the agent. That is what the copies will leave out.

Hidden gem

Needham Monastery

The years will be superb and small. Translate them into a language a larger employer understands — a decision log, a measured impact, a prototype someone outside Needham used — or the education stays private.

Worst case

Exhibit

You will make beautiful artifacts and be asked for very little responsibility. Build a trail of decisions: what you tried, what you refused, what broke. If the portfolio is only objects, you used Olin as a shop class.

Do this while you are here

  • Defend every major project orally, even when not required.
  • Work with a real user who can reject the work.
  • Learn enough craft that you can tell when the agent is wrong.
  • Keep a decision log. That log is the portfolio if artifacts become cheap.
