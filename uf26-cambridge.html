Board two-pager

For the Vice-Chancellor and Council

Crux

Convert national-lab compute and corridor money into a university that still funds and houses discovery people — without becoming a science park that outsourced the collegiate teaching engine, or a US-refugee campus that forgot the North-East of England.

Diagnosis in six lines

Cambridge is a collegiate research university of about 24,900 students, 31 colleges, and a science cluster the UK government now treats as industrial policy. Forty-two percent of students are overseas; sixty-two percent of postgraduates are. That mix is the engine. Prentice’s 2025 address named the three things scholars need: freedom, time, stability. Compute arrived faster than those three. Dawn is being scaled sixfold; Zenith launched in June 2026 with AMD and a DSIT minister in the room; Sunrise is the fusion machine with UKAEA; SAIL is the public-private software layer with AMD and Dell. The Rokos School of Government, announced March 2026 on a £190 million gift, is the humanities-and-power bid. The OxCam corridor is the geography bid. The next fundraising campaign is supposed to fully fund PhD places. That last sentence is the tell. Faculty already ration admits they cannot pay for. International-fee income was £192 million in 2024–25 — 14 percent of a £1.4 billion university. A levy and a shorter graduate visa are not weather. They are a second budget. Separately, the University is sitting with the death of Professor Jason Arday and a review Prentice herself commissioned. Governance capacity is not infinite. Machines do not spend it. People cases do.

Guiding policy

People before petaflops. Attach graduate seats and supervision capacity to every named machine and school. Treat the corridor as a labour-market for Cambridge ideas, not as a substitute for a funded doctoral body. Keep the collegiate undergraduate as a designed product for a thinner graduate layer. Spend presidential attention on the PhD floor and on whether Cambridge is still visitable from more than one political weather system.

Coherent actions

  1. Publish a three-year doctoral-funding floor by school: no unfunded admits hiding inside faculty caution. Make the next campaign’s PhD goal a Council metric, not a slogan.
  2. Convert a defined share of Dawn / Zenith / SAIL / Rokos operating money into studentship and postdoc years, not only PI time and vendors.
  3. Redesign undergraduate assessment and supervision load for a world with fewer graduate teachers. Supervisions that assume 2019 density will silently degrade first.
  4. Price the levy and visa shock at Council: which experiments stop if international PGT income falls 15 percent. Do not wait for the Home Office to do the strategy.

Start

A three-year doctoral-funding floor by school. Convert a defined share of Dawn/Zenith/SAIL/Rokos money into studentship years. Price the levy and visa shock at Council.

Stop

Unfunded admits hiding inside faculty caution. Supervisions that assume 2019 density. Waiting for the Home Office before doing the arithmetic.

Go

Supervision plus Tripos. Collegiate science. Adapt the graduate-teacher supply; do not abandon the supervision.

Full report

What Cambridge is

Cambridge is not Oxford with a different river. It is a tighter science-and-collegiate hybrid sitting on a cluster the state has decided to treat as a growth engine. Undergraduates about 12,850; postgraduates about 12,080. Academic staff 35 percent overseas; research staff 57 percent. That is a world gathering place that happens to be in the Fens, not a British university that admits some foreigners.

Prentice came from Princeton and has not tried to make Cambridge into Princeton. She has tried to make it a place that can still offer freedom, time, and stability while the UK asks it to carry quantum, fusion, AI-for-science, a new school of government, and a better relationship with India — CBSE Class XII now accepted for some courses, a Section 8 company for Indian philanthropy, a Cambridge-India Centre for Advanced Studies. Useful. Secondary.

Forces that actually move this institution

  • Compute as industrial policy. AIRR, DSIT, UKRI, AMD, Dell, IonQ, UKAEA. Cambridge is being recapitalised as a sovereign science platform. Platforms need operators. Operators are graduate students and research staff on visas.
  • The broken cross-subsidy. Home undergraduate education does not pay for itself. International fees and research overheads do. Levy plus shorter graduate-route visas hit both the cash and the people.
  • PhD as infrastructure. Prentice has already said the next campaign will aim to fully fund offered doctorates. That is an admission that the current system rations in the dark.
  • Cluster versus university. The corridor wants productivity and exports. Colleges want supervisions. Those can align. They do not align automatically.
  • Governance load. Arday is not a strategy issue and must not be treated as one. It is a reminder that attention is scarce and that “more science” does not dissolve a people problem.
“What scholars and students need to do their work is three things: freedom, time, and stability.” — Deborah Prentice, 1 October 2025

Crux

Keep a research-and-teaching university running when the state is buying machines faster than it is buying the people who use them, and when the visa office can veto the postgraduate majority. Named schools and supercomputers do not replace a missing cohort. Honest studentship floors and an undergraduate model that does not assume 2019 graduate density do.

Kernel Reconstruction, September 2026 — not Cambridge’s published plan

Diagnosis

Cambridge is being offered a national-lab future on attractive terms. The hidden invoice is a thinner doctoral body, a more British campus, and a collegiate undergraduate that quietly loses its teachers.

Guiding policy

Attach people to every machine. Floor PhD funding in the open. Redesign supervision for a thinner graduate layer. Treat corridor money as a labour market for ideas, not as a substitute for a university.

Coherent actions

Doctoral funding floors by school. Studentships inside Dawn/Zenith/SAIL/Rokos. Levy-and-visa scenario at Council. Undergraduate assessment that survives fewer supervisors. Stop announcing boxes until the last three have named seats.

Critical uncertainties

Axis A — The sovereign AI-for-science bargain. Compute arrives with people and problem choice, or compute arrives as boxes and contractors.

Axis B — Talent geography. The world still sends its best to the Fens, or talent fragments toward firms, other hubs, and a more American-British campus.

A people · B gathering

Fen laboratory

2030

Dawn, Zenith and SAIL have named studentships attached to them, so a new PhD offer comes with four years of money rather than a shrug. Corridor firms hire Cambridge people without emptying the colleges — a supervision still happens twice a week in the core Tripos papers. India and US inflows offset a softer China taught-graduate market. An undergraduate notices the machines as extra kit, not as a reason there is no demonstrator in the lab.

2040

Cambridge is the UK’s discovery capital and still a world campus: more than half the research staff hold a non-UK passport, and hall still mixes systems. The 2025–26 scare — compute arriving faster than seats — is taught as a case other civic universities cannot copy because they do not have this cluster. A hiring panel in Boston still treats a Cambridge doctorate as the same species as an MIT one.

A people · B fragments

British core, thinner rim

2030

Money and seats exist; passports do not, not at the old ratios. Some wet labs and some language faculties thin first because they lived on overseas graduate teachers. An undergraduate in a popular Tripos still gets supervisions; an undergraduate in a small language paper gets a video and a packed class. Council calls this ‘sustainable internationalisation.’ It is a different mix.

2040

A more British, more industrial Cambridge. Still strong in quantum, fusion and AI-for-science. Different species from the 2010s gathering place. Foreign faculties treat it as a national lab with a famous undergraduate brand rather than as a peer that sends and receives people in both directions.

A boxes · B gathering

Park with a brand

2030

Talent still comes for the name. Problem choice quietly follows sponsors and agencies: the interesting PhD is the one Dawn’s operating grant will pay for. Supervisions get shorter because graduate teachers are on secondment to the cluster. An undergraduate can spend three years adjacent to world-class machines and never be asked a question the sponsor did not already like.

2040

The valuable Cambridge is the cluster — the firms, the boxes, the corridor. The University is a residential front end on a national lab. Colleges still photograph well. The degree certifies that you were selected at 18 and housed near the park.

A boxes · B fragments

Smaller, harder Cambridge

2030

Both people and boxes shrink. Departments merge programmes. Undergraduate Cambridge becomes more tutorial for some — the colleges that can still pay — and more video for others. A graduate applicant is told the truth in the offer letter: funded, or do not come.

2040

A smaller University that kept its standards and lost its scale. Excellent if Council chose the shrinkage and floored the remaining seats. Ugly if it happened by visa and levy while the press office still announced machines.

Implications

Policy that only works in “Fen laboratory” is not a policy. Funding floors and a supervision model that does not assume 2019 density help in all four worlds. Deep exclusive industry platforms help cash in “park with a brand” and hurt independence in the same world. Council should treat “smaller, harder Cambridge” as a design option, not only as a failure mode.

A bad strategy would be: launch another named machine, hold the corridor announcement as if it were a budget, and let doctoral shrinkage happen as eighty separate faculty decisions.

For students · Cambridge

The supercomputer will not mark your supervision. A missing PhD student will.

If you are an undergraduate, the missing graduates show up as thinner supervisions and slower labs. If you are a graduate student, funding is the first fact, not the last. Plan for the University you are in, not the one in the 2019 film.

Best case

Fen laboratory

Machines and people together. Rare. Do not budget your twenties on rare.

Likely-comfortable

Park with a brand

Plenty of sponsor-shaped problems. Ask who owns the question before you join the group.

Different country

Thinner rim

Money without the old mix of passports. Your network will not build itself in hall.

Hard mode

Smaller Cambridge

Fewer seats, higher standards, less slack. Excellent if you wanted intensity. Bad if you wanted a large scene.

Do this while you are here

  • Start Pick supervisors by funding horizon, not only by citation count.
  • Start Learn in labs and colleges that still have humans in the room. That capacity is what gets cut first.
  • Start Build one serious collaboration outside Cambridge. Magnetism is no longer a law of nature.
  • Go Use Dawn and Zenith if they serve your problem. Do not confuse access to a box with a research life.
  • Stop Stop assuming a supervision will appear at 2019 density. Book the hours while they exist.
