How each pack is built

Book A is Rumelt, Good Strategy/Bad Strategy (2011). Book B is Rumelt, The Crux (2022). Scenarios are a 2×2 on two critical uncertainties, written at 2030 and again at 2040. The kernel is a reconstruction dated September 2026. It is not the university’s published strategy dressed up as analysis.

The kernel

A kernel has three parts that have to fit.

  1. Diagnosis. What is going on — the pattern, not the press release. A diagnosis that could be pasted onto any peer has failed.
  2. Guiding policy. The approach for resolving the crux. Not a motto. Not “excellence, impact, community.” If you can hang it on a banner, it is not a policy.
  3. Coherent actions. A short set of moves that only make sense together because they all push on the same difficulty. A to-do list is not coherence.

The crux

The crux is the most important difficulty that is also addressable from the president’s chair in the next few years. Importance without addressability is a weather report. Addressability without importance is busywork. If the diagnosis is sound, a crux appears. If you cannot name one, the diagnosis is still a catalogue.

Scenarios

Each university gets two axes that are uncertain, consequential, and partly outside its control. Four named worlds. Each world is described at 2030 (the planning horizon of a sitting board) and 2040 (when the undergraduate admitted in 2026 is in mid-career and the faculty hired now have tenure). Watchpoints are observables, not hopes.

Start · Stop · Go

Every kernel action is tagged so a board or a student can see whether thinking has to shift, drop, or keep going.

  • Start — begin it because a new opportunity exists, or because what used to work will be increasingly challenged. New work, or a replacement for a challenged habit.
  • Stop — drop it because the old practice no longer produces the scarce product. Not “do less of.” Stop.
  • Go — continue what already works. Adapt the instrument if you must. Do not abandon the job.

Start / Stop / Go labels the kernel — present-tense policy. The four worlds test that policy; they do not each get their own to-do list. If a world arrives that kills a Go, that is a watchpoint, not a second strategy.

Three cuts, later a score

The 30 packs can be read as a roster, or as three overlapping maps: what must stay scarce (A), who writes the problem list (B), and which weather system the campus sits in (C). A university belongs in more than one. That is the point. A later Bayesian layer can attach probabilities to the 2030 worlds and update cousins together. See the clusters page. No probabilities live in these files yet.

What to mark as you read

  • Is the crux actually addressable, or did we name the weather?
  • Are the two axes the right uncertainties, or the fashionable ones?
  • Does the student view say something a communications office would not already print?
  • Do adjacent universities in the same system or city still disagree with each other?
