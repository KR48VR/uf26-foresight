Massachusetts Institute of Technology · Scratch rebuild · 20 September 2026

The unit of production at MIT is the funded doctoral student. That unit shrank in 2026, and industry money will not automatically replace it.

Sally Kornbluth told the campus in May: federal awards down more than 20 percent, total sponsored research down 10 percent, new graduate enrollment outside Sloan and the EECS MEng down nearly 20 percent — about 500 fewer students. The Institute rejected the White House compact in October 2025 on merit-funding and independence grounds. Independence is expensive. The crux is how MIT rebuilds the lab without letting the replacement sponsor choose the problem.

Board two-pager

For the President and the MIT Corporation

Crux

Rebuild the production system of MIT science — a funded doctoral student attached to a faculty problem — on a sponsor mix that is no longer majority-federal, without surrendering problem choice to companies, philanthropies, or a conditional compact with Washington.

Diagnosis in six lines

MIT’s distinctive engine was not undergraduate prestige and not the endowment. It was a dense mesh of federally funded labs in which a graduate student could spend five or six years on a problem that did not yet have a customer. In May 2026 that mesh contracted in public: federal research activity down more than 20 percent year-on-year; new federal awards down by a similar amount; combined sponsored research down 10 percent; departments cutting incoming doctoral cohorts by about a fifth. The 8 percent endowment tax costs roughly $240 million a year on a $1.7 billion operating budget. A four-year visa cap, even temporarily blocked in September 2026, has already changed the calculation for international PhD applicants. Industry and gifts are growing. They are not growing as a substitute for curiosity-driven work. They grow as a substitute for work that can be specified in a statement of work.

Guiding policy

Treat the doctoral student as the scarce capital asset. Ring-fence a smaller, explicitly funded core of unowned problems. Use industry and philanthropy to scale platforms and translation only where faculty still choose the question. Keep international doctoral intake even when Washington makes it politically expensive. Do not trade the compact-rejection letter for a quieter version of the same deal two years later.

Coherent actions

  1. Publish an Institute-wide “unowned research” floor: a dollar and FTE target, by school, that cannot be booked against a corporate SOW or a mission-directed gift.
  2. Stabilize doctoral intake with a central matching pool so departments stop rationing offers against last quarter’s grant cash — and publish the intake numbers every cycle.
  3. Build multi-year visa and stipend insurance for international PhDs (legal defense, bridge funding, partner-lab fallback outside the US) as a recruiting product, not a hardship program.
  4. Put Climate Project, Schwarzman College, and MITHIC under the same problem-choice rule: platforms may take industry scale; the questions may not be sold.
  5. Refuse any federal bargain that conditions award flow on enrollment mix, speech codes, or geography-weighted merit. Keep a written alternative funding path ready so the refusal is not a bluff.

Start

An Institute-wide unowned-research floor in dollars and FTE that cannot be booked against a corporate SOW. A central doctoral matching pool with published intake. Multi-year visa and stipend insurance for international PhDs.

Stop

Trading the compact-rejection letter for a quieter version of the same deal. Rationing offers against last quarter’s grant cash. Letting industry buy the question.

Go

Treat the doctoral student as the scarce capital asset. Keep international intake even when it is politically expensive. Use industry money for platforms and translation only where faculty still choose the problem.

Full report

What MIT is, in 2026

A private research institute in Cambridge whose global position was built on a seventy-year bargain: Washington paid for curiosity-driven work; MIT trained the people who could do it; companies and national labs harvested the overflow. Undergraduate education is excellent and not the constraint. Sloan is healthy and not the constraint. The constraint is the lab.

Kornbluth’s May 2026 campus message is the diagnosis the Institute itself will now have to live with. Congress restored some agency budgets. The money is not arriving as awards. Investigators are writing into a void. Departments, rationally, admitted fewer students. That rationality aggregates into a pipeline cut.

Forces that actually move this institution

  • Federal bargain under conditionality. New awards down more than 20 percent. Geography-weighted allocation has been discussed at agencies. The White House compact offered restored flow in exchange for a 15 percent undergraduate international cap and limits on institutional independence. MIT rejected it first, on 10 October 2025, on merit-funding grounds. Rejection is a policy. It is also a revenue decision.
  • Endowment tax as a structural tax on reserves. FY2025 endowment $27.4 billion after a 14.8 percent return. The 8 percent tax on earnings — MIT is in the top bracket with Harvard, Princeton, Yale, Stanford — is budgeted around $240 million a year. That is not a rounding error on a $1.7 billion budget. It is a standing claim on the only flexible money that could backfill grants.
  • International doctoral logistics. Kornbluth called a four-year visa cap “untenable for PhD students” in July 2026. A federal judge blocked the rule in mid-September, one day before it would have taken effect. Blocked is not repealed. Yield among the best 22-year-olds is already being made in Singapore, Zurich, Toronto, and Beijing.
  • Industry as a growing but agenda-setting sponsor. Non-federal research is up. It does not offset the federal drop. It also arrives with a problem list. Schwarzman College, the Climate Project (six missions, $75 million initial Institute pledge), and a thickening set of corporate labs on and near campus are the attractive version of the same risk.
  • Internal hedges that are not yet a system. MITHIC is trying to keep SHASS in the conversation. Climate Project is trying to convert lab work into missions. Neither replaces 500 missing graduate students.

Crux

Keep a production system in which a doctoral student can spend six years on a problem that does not yet have a buyer — after the historic buyer stepped back and the replacement buyers have product roadmaps.

Kernel Reconstruction, 20 September 2026 — not the published strategy

Diagnosis

MIT is not short of brand, applicants, or near-campus capital. It is short of the specific combination that made it MIT: a federally funded student, a faculty-chosen problem, and time. Substituting industry scale without protecting problem choice produces a very good contract research organization with a famous undergraduate college attached.

Guiding policy

Defend the unowned problem as a budget line, not a slogan. Recruit and retain the international doctorate as infrastructure. Take industry money for platforms. Do not take it for questions.

Coherent actions

An unowned-research floor with public accounting. A central doctoral matching pool. Visa-and-stipend insurance as a recruiting product. One problem-choice rule across Climate, Schwarzman, and MITHIC. A written non-federal fallback so compact-refusal remains solvent.

Critical uncertainties

Axis A — Who pays for discovery. A restored merit-based federal compact, or a durable shift to conditional public money plus industry statements of work.

Axis B — Where the world’s best 22-year-olds do a doctorate. They still come to Cambridge, or they reroute to Europe, Singapore, Canada, and a tightening set of Chinese and Korean programs.

Merit federal · Talent stays

Second Compact

2030

Awards recover on merit terms. Doctoral intake is back near 2024. Climate missions and Schwarzman look like additions, not substitutions. MIT’s refusal of the 2025 compact is remembered as the opening bid that held.

2040

Cambridge is still the default place to train a discovery scientist in the English-speaking world. The endowment tax is a nuisance, not the business model.

Merit federal · Talent reroutes

American Core

2030

Money is back. People are not. Labs run on a more domestic, slightly smaller doctorate. Quality holds in a few departments and thins in the rest.

2040

MIT is a national champion with a narrower accent. The global graduate commons has moved east and north.

Industrial paymaster · Talent stays

Sponsored Institute

2030

The best students still come because the brand and the companies are here. Their dissertations read like well-written internships. Faculty calendars fill with sponsor reviews.

2040

MIT is the most prestigious corporate lab in America that still grants degrees. Undergraduates are fine. Basic science has a museum wing.

Boutique MIT

2030

Awards stay tight, international yield falls, and departments shrink around a few star PIs with private money. The Climate Project becomes the public face because the lab floor is quieter.

2040

A beloved, smaller Institute. The production system that minted mid-career scientists for the world has moved. Kendall Square still sparkles.

Implications for the sitting Corporation

The bad strategy is to wait for Washington to become itself again while quietly filling the hole with named labs. That path produces the Sponsored Institute even if everyone involved is well-intentioned. The robust strategy is to make unowned research countable, doctoral intake a Corporation-level number, and international logistics a product MIT is willing to spend endowment income on — tax included.

Climate Project and MITHIC are useful only if they are not asked to do the work of the missing 500 students. They can change what MIT is for. They cannot replace who does the work.

For students · MIT

Your advisor’s next grant is now part of your education. So is the possibility it does not arrive.

MIT is still a place where a hard problem can eat five years of your life. In 2026 that is a scarcer offer than the admissions brochure implies. Use the scarcity.

Best case

Second Compact

Federal money returns on merit. Take the unowned problem anyway. That habit travels.

Narrower bench

American Core

Fewer international classmates. Learn to be fluent outside the US research system; you may need it.

Sponsor gravity

Sponsored Institute

Great facilities, thinner questions. Keep one project that has no customer.

Worst case

Boutique MIT

A famous, smaller lab. Your network and your unpublished work matter more than the letterhead.

Do this while you are here

  • Start Ask, before you join a group: what share of this lab’s work would exist if the current sponsor vanished?
  • Start Learn to write a proposal, not only a paper. That skill is the new literacy.
  • Go Keep one collaboration off campus and, if you can, off-country.
  • Start If you are international, treat visa logistics as part of the research plan, not an afterthought.
  • Go Use Schwarzman and Climate Project as platforms. Do not let them become the only questions you know how to ask.
  • Stop Stop booking your dissertation against a sponsor’s statement of work if you can still choose the question.
