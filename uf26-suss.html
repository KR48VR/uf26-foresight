Singapore University of Social Sciences · Scratch rebuild · September 2026

SUSS is about to get a city campus and a 40,000-seat ambition. The crux is whether that makes it a university of social good — or a very large landlord for adult classes.

Tan Tai Yong has run SUSS since 2023. The government announced a Rochor city campus sized for 40,000 full-time and part-time students. Singapore turned super-aged in 2026. The brand refresh is “Dream, Dare, Do.” A campus can concentrate a university, or freeze a delivery operation that still thinks like an open university with better interiors.

Board two-pager

For the President and Board of Trustees

Crux

Build Rochor as a civic university for a super-aged, AI-disrupted workforce — measured by changed practice in care, work and community — not by filling 40,000 seats.

Diagnosis in six lines

SUSS does not have a permanent campus today. It rents, including at SIM Clementi. Its real market is working adults and career-switchers, not the 18-year-old A-level pipeline NUS and NTU own. That is a strategic gift in an AI labour market that will force mid-career rewrite. It is also why SUSS is easy for the rest of the system to patronise. Detectors were retired in 2026 — the right call for adult learners. Applied research in gerontology and social good is present but not yet funded like a professional school funds a clinic. Without a sharp practice spine, Rochor becomes floor space.

Guiding policy

Agenda before architecture. Design the campus for evening and hybrid gravity. Hire practitioner-scholars who can run clinics, not only modules. Do not let 40,000 become a growth KPI.

Coherent actions

  1. Lock a research-and-practice agenda before the building: ageing and care, mid-career work, community resilience.
  2. Design Rochor for night and hybrid. Adult learners are the majority, not an add-on.
  3. Refuse seat-count as strategy. Quality of practice partnerships first.
  4. Hire practitioner-scholars as a first-class track and promote them.
  5. Keep andragogy as a published method and test it against AI-era work.

Full report

What it is

SUSS is Singapore’s applied social-sciences and lifelong-learning university. It is the institution the system uses when it wants mass upskilling without pretending every learner is 19. That role gets more important as careers fragment. It only becomes a university if knowledge is made there, not just distributed.

Forces that actually move this institution

  • Super-aged Singapore from 2026 — care, longevity markets, family systems.
  • AI rewrite of mid-career white-collar work — SUSS’s actual students live this.
  • Rochor campus as the first permanent home.
  • Competition from polytechnics, private providers, and NUS/NTU continuing-ed arms.

Practice · adult-first

Civic Engine

2030

A nurse, a social worker and a career-switcher can all point to a SUSS supervisor who saw them practice, not just mark a paper. Rochor is full after 6pm because the timetable was built for employed students first. Employers send staff on purpose. Success is counted as job change, licence or wage band at 12 months, not as certificates issued.

2040

SUSS is how Singapore retrains a mid-career workforce without pretending everyone can pause life for three years at Kent Ridge. Other ageing societies send people to copy the practice sites, not the branding.

Practice · day-campus

Institute in the Wrong Building

2030

Full-time undergraduates dominate the story and the timetable. Adult learners are fitted around them. Research groups look respectable. A working student still feels like a guest after 6pm.

2040

A small comprehensive with evening leftovers. The charter and the calendar do not match. Polytechnics took back the employed student.

Utility · adult-first

SkillsFuture Hall

2030

Volume is high. Short courses pay. Practice supervision is thin. A student can collect modules that do not change Monday morning. The university measures certificates issued.

2040

A respected funding channel and a weak university. People come for the subsidy, not for the teaching.

Utility · day-campus

Empty Evenings

2030

Adult numbers stall; the day campus is half a story. Prestige faculties that needed research-intensive staff SUSS could not hire sit half-empty. The employed student went elsewhere.

2040

A quiet campus whose original purpose has moved to polytechnics and private providers.

Implications

The Board’s job between now and opening is to refuse seat-count as strategy. Pick three practice fields and fund them like professional schools.

A bad strategy would be: treat 40,000 seats as the victory condition and let the building programme stand in for a research agenda.

For students · SUSS

You are not a second-choice undergraduate. You are the point.

SUSS is built for people who already have a job, a family, or a first qualification that did not stick. It is not a quieter NUS. The advantage is that your workplace can be the laboratory — but only if someone there supervises you by name, and only if the timetable is built around employed students rather than 19-year-olds.

Best case for you

Civic Engine

A nurse, social worker or career-switcher can point to a SUSS supervisor who saw them practice, not just mark a paper. Your workplace problem becomes a method other people in the cohort use. Employers send staff on purpose.

Awkward fit

Wrong Building

Full-time undergraduates dominate the story. Adult learners are fitted around them. Good ideas, a campus timed for someone else.

Volume, thin practice

SkillsFuture Hall

You collect subsidised short courses. Practice supervision is thin. You upskill; the university does not change what you can do on Monday.

Worst case

Empty Evenings

You feel like an afterthought on a youth campus. The original purpose has moved to polytechnics and private providers.

Do this while you are here

  • If you work, pick the programme that already has a practice site in your sector. Ask who supervises you on the job, by name.
  • Bring a live problem from work or family care into the capstone. An abstract essay wastes this university.
  • Find one practitioner-scholar and stay with them. Treat classmates’ jobs as a second faculty.
  • Do not collect SkillsFuture modules that do not change your Monday. If you are 19 and here for a quieter NUS, you are in the wrong plot.
