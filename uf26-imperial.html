Imperial College London · September 2026

Imperial already lives on overseas fees and White City tenants. “Science for humanity” is a purpose. It is not yet a funding model.

Eighty percent of tuition income is overseas. Headcount fell for the first time in twenty years. Brady is asking the campus to deepen international alliances and, separately, to discuss defence. Those two sentences can both be true. They are not the same strategy.

Board two-pager

For the President and Council

Crux

Stay a world STEM university when four-fifths of fee income is overseas and the UK is taxing that flow — without selling problem choice to White City tenants or to the Ministry of Defence, and without letting assessment lag the AI vision document.

Diagnosis in six lines

Imperial is a specialised STEM-and-business university in west London that learned how to look like a global scientific power from a thin public grant. In 2024–25 total income was £1.49 billion. Overseas tuition fees were £408 million — 80.7 percent of all fee income, up from 78.8 percent. Home fees do not carry this place. The first headcount decline in twenty years arrived in the same accounts: 23,248 students against 23,311. Brady’s strategy, Science for Humanity, is the moral frame: Imperial Talent, Imperial Futures (convergence science), Imperial Innovates. White City is the physical frame: deep-tech campus, incubator firms that have raised hundreds of millions, professional services already moved off South Kensington to free teaching space. A Vision for Education in the Age of AI was consulted in early 2026 and human-centred on the page. A UK Centre for AI-Driven Innovation was launched at Davos in January 2026. Scholarships were launched in Singapore in July. In March 2026 Brady told the campus to resist isolationism — and, in the same season, that government, industry and society expect Imperial to contribute to UK security and defence. The crux is not whether Imperial does AI. It is whether the cash engine (overseas STEM students) and the problem-choice engine (faculty plus White City plus MoD) still make one university.

Guiding policy

Diversify the fee engine before the levy and the visa office do it for you. Keep White City as a place where Imperial owns the question, not as a science park that rents the brand. Write defence principles in public before the first large contract writes them in private. Make the AI education vision visible in finals, not in a PDF.

Coherent actions

  1. A three-year international-mix plan with named alternative markets and a hard cap on any single-country share of fee income. India is already called “strategic.” Call the concentration risk by its real name: China-plus-one is not a slogan if the numbers do not move.
  2. A problem-choice rule for White City and for defence work: which questions stay faculty-led, which are co-owned, which are refused. Publish the rule. Silence is also a policy.
  3. Assessment redesign in every faculty against the AI vision: live, lab, oral, closed-book foundations. The Union already signed the vision. Use that.
  4. Price a 15 percent overseas-fee shock at Council, including which White City phases slip. Philanthropy doubled to £75 million. It does not replace £408 million.

Full report

What Imperial is

Imperial is not a comprehensive university. It is a STEM-medicine-business institute that uses London as a magnet and White City as a second campus for translation. About 12,100 full-time undergraduates, 9,400 full-time postgraduates, 1,700 part-time. UK students are 39 percent of headcount; China 23 percent; India 4 percent. That distribution is the strategy, whether or not it is written as one.

Forces that actually move this institution

  • Fee geography. Overseas students pay for the home teaching Imperial pretends is publicly funded. Levy, visa refusals, and a shorter graduate route hit Imperial harder than a civic university with a different mix — and harder than Oxford, which has college endowments to lean on.
  • White City as species change. A deep-tech campus with 100-plus firms is an asset if Imperial still assigns the problems. It is a different institution if the firms do.
  • Isolationism versus defence. Brady is right that international science is under pressure. He is also opening a conversation about security work. Those can be complementary. They can also split the faculty and the international classroom.
  • Assessment lag. The AI vision is careful and union-endorsed. Literacy is easy. Changing what an Imperial degree certifies is faculty-intensive and unrewarded.
  • Provost transition. Walmsley announced his departure in 2025. Strategy documents outlive provosts only if Council treats them as constraints, not as magazines.
“Yes, the tide of isolationism is strong. But by working with like-minded trusted partners, we can turn it.” — Hugh Brady, President’s Address, 11 March 2026

Kernel Reconstruction, September 2026 — not Imperial’s published plan

Diagnosis

Imperial is a high-quality STEM institute whose variable income is a geopolitical instrument and whose physical growth strategy (White City) can quietly reassign problem choice.

Guiding policy

De-concentrate the fee engine. Own the question at White City and in defence. Make the AI vision show up in exams. Carry internationalism as a costed position, not a speech.

Coherent actions

Country-mix cap and India-plus plan. Published problem-choice rule. Assessment redesign against the vision. A Council shock scenario for overseas fees. Stop using Davos launches as proof of strategy.

Critical uncertainties

Axis A — International STEM talent to London. The levy and visa system stay livable, or they cut the pipeline Imperial actually runs on.

Axis B — Who owns the question. Faculty-led convergence science, or a branded park plus defence contractor.

A holds · B faculty

South Kensington global

2030

The overseas mix is less dependent on one country: India and a second Asian market have moved the numbers, and a published cap on any single-country share is a Council metric. White City translates Imperial science rather than assigning it. Finals in the core STEM courses include a closed-book foundation paper and a lab or oral the model cannot sit. Defence work exists inside a published rule about which questions stay faculty-led.

2040

Imperial is the UK’s world STEM university and still looks like one from Beijing, Bombay and Boston. A graduate can move between systems. The College did not become a science park that happens to matriculate students, and it did not become a ministry lab that happens to teach.

A holds · B tenants

White City plc

2030

Students still come. Problems follow tenants and agencies: the interesting project is the one the incubator firm or the sponsor already wanted. The degree is a ticket into the park. Assessment looks modern and is mostly take-home. Brady’s internationalism is a speech; the operating system is the tenant mix.

2040

A peerless industrial research college. Independence is a legal office. Foreign faculties still cite Imperial papers; they hire from the firms on the park more than from the departments. South Kensington is the brand layer.

A cuts · B faculty

Smaller, harder Imperial

2030

Overseas fees shrink after the levy and a shorter graduate visa. Headcount is designed down rather than denied in the dark. Quality holds in fewer programmes; a couple of taught master’s close. Faculty own the remaining questions. An undergraduate notices smaller labs and harder exams, not a collapse.

2040

A leaner Institute that kept standards and lost scale. Survivable because Council chose which programmes to keep and published the shock scenario before the Home Office wrote it. Not the 2010s growth story. Still a university.

A cuts · B tenants

Contract campus

2030

Both cash and questions move off the faculty balance sheet. Taught programmes shrink as overseas fees fall, and the work that remains is tenant- and defence-shaped. A student collects modules for a contractor. Switchability of vendors is a slogan; the stack is someone else’s.

2040

The brand remains. The university is a contractor with lecture theatres. White City’s firms and a handful of defence programmes are the going concern. A hiring panel treats the Imperial MSc as a park internship with a transcript.

Implications

Country-mix discipline and published rails help in every world except “contract campus,” where Council has already lost. White City density without a problem-choice rule helps only “White City plc.” Designed small is better than accidental small.

A bad strategy would be: another Davos centre, a quiet defence portfolio, 2023 exams with an integrity paragraph, and hope that China and India keep sending full-fee students as if 2019 were a law of nature.

For students · Imperial

The College can teach you to build the system. It cannot, by itself, tell you whose question you are answering.

White City will tempt you with firms. South Kensington will tempt you with prestige. The useful question is whether you can still do the work when the model is shut off, and whether the problem you joined was yours.

Best case

South Kensington global

Harder exams. Real labs. A two-facing network. Worth the Tube.

Comfortable trap

White City plc

Great internships. Someone else’s roadmap. Ask who owns the question.

Hard mode

Smaller Imperial

Fewer seats, less slack, high standards. Excellent if you wanted intensity.

Worst case

Contract campus

You collected modules for a contractor. Build something outside the transcript.

Do this while you are here

  • Take the live, lab, and oral assessments seriously. They are the part of Imperial that will still mean something.
  • Read the sponsorship of your group before you join it. Defence and tenants are not automatically bad. Unnamed rails are.
  • Do not treat China-heavy classrooms as the world. Build one network that is not your largest nationality group.
  • Use White City as an instrument. Do not confuse proximity to a startup with a research life.
