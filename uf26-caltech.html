California Institute of Technology · Scratch rebuild · 20 September 2026

Caltech’s advantage is that almost no one works there. The new president has to defend that advantage while JPL, quantum, and AI all demand scale.

Ray Jayawardhana became the tenth president on 1 July 2026, succeeding Thomas Rosenbaum. The Ginsburg Center for Quantum Precision Measurement was dedicated in June. Manuel Endres’s group trapped 6,100 neutral-atom qubits. The endowment is about $4.4 billion. None of that changes the arithmetic: this is a 2,200-person institute that also manages NASA’s planetary robotics lab. Small is the strategy. Small is also the exposure.

Board two-pager

For the President and the Board of Trustees

Crux

Caltech has about 2,200 students, a new president as of July 2026, a new quantum building, and responsibility for JPL. Every one of those facts pulls toward “just a little larger.” The hard problem is to stay small enough that a faculty meeting is still an argument about a measurement — and to say, in Jayawardhana’s first year, which growth Caltech will refuse so that Ginsburg, JPL and the undergraduate house system do not quietly become three different institutes sharing a zip code.

Diagnosis in six lines

Caltech wins by refusal: few students, few departments, long bets, a faculty that can still fit in one auditorium. That refusal is under three simultaneous pressures. JPL is a national planetary-robotics instrument whose budget and political weather are not set in Pasadena. Quantum has just acquired a building (Ginsburg Center) and a result (6,100-qubit array) that will attract money that wants a product roadmap. Industry is already on campus — Meta’s wearable lab among the new footprints — and student culture is arguing about whether frontier-lab sponsorship of a math hackathon is compatible with the discipline. Jayawardhana inherits Rosenbaum’s physical plant and not yet a written rule for what Caltech will not become.

Guiding policy

Small is a research-design choice, not a brand. Grow instruments, not headcount. Take JPL and industry as unique apparatus, not as a second faculty. Publish the list of things Caltech will not staff.

Coherent actions

  1. Write a three-page “what we will not build” memo in the first year: no professional schools, no undergraduate expansion past a published cap, no sponsored center whose problem list is set off-campus.
  2. Treat JPL as a scientific instrument with a firewall: Caltech faculty time on JPL problems is a privilege with a published fraction, not an unbounded service obligation.
  3. Put Ginsburg and the Endres-class quantum work on a discovery budget, with a separate, smaller translation budget that industry may fund.
  4. Set a sponsorship code for student research events before the next Mathathon cycle — so the Institute is not improvising after 771 mathematicians write a letter.
  5. Use the new presidency to recruit five to eight mid-career faculty in the Institute’s actual bottlenecks (quantum measurement, planetary instrumentation, AI-for-science), not to add adjacent fields that larger universities already cover.

Start

A three-page “what we will not build” memo in year one. JPL time as a published fraction, not an unbounded service obligation. Ginsburg and Endres-class quantum on a discovery budget, translation booked separately.

Stop

Professional schools. Undergraduate expansion past a published cap. A sponsored centre whose problem list is set off-campus.

Go

Small as a research-design choice. Grow instruments, not headcount. JPL and industry as unique apparatus, not a second faculty.

Full report

What Caltech is

The smallest serious science institute in the United States, sitting next to a NASA center it manages, in a city that is also a neighborhood of Los Angeles capital. Its published claim is fundamental discovery. Its unpublished claim is that a faculty of this size can still choose problems together.

Forces that actually move this institution

  • Leadership reset. Jayawardhana, an astrophysicist and former Johns Hopkins provost, arrived 1 July 2026. He has said fundamental discovery and applied innovation are not in tension. That sentence is true at Caltech only if someone guards the tension.
  • Quantum becoming a campus, not a group. Ginsburg Center dedicated 17 June 2026 (70,000 sq ft). Endres et al., Nature 2025: 6,100 cesium-atom qubits, 13-second coherence, 99.98 percent operational accuracy. That result pulls facilities money, students, and companies that will not be satisfied with a paper.
  • JPL as gift and hostage. Planetary robotics is a Caltech-shaped public good. It is also a federal weather system. A president who “advances JPL” without a campus firewall will spend the Institute’s scarce faculty attention on someone else’s mission directorate.
  • Industry on the lawn. FY2025 report: Meta partnership and an on-campus wearables lab; endowment investments ~$4.4 billion as of 30 September 2025. Useful. Not free.
  • Culture of refusal, newly tested. OpenAI withdrew from the student-led Mathathon on 10 September 2026 after 771 mathematicians objected. The event proceeds. The Institute still needs a rule, not a news cycle.
“I intend to be a fierce advocate for the Institute’s mission… that fundamental discovery and applied innovation are not in tension, that they reinforce each other.” — Ray Jayawardhana, 2026

Crux

Defend small as a constraint that produces better science — while operating one of the country’s most scale-hungry labs next door and standing up a quantum building that industry already knows how to colonize.

Kernel Reconstruction, 20 September 2026 — not the published strategy

Diagnosis

Caltech’s scarcity is the point. Every successful year makes scarcity harder to defend, because success arrives as buildings, partnerships, and JPL tasking. A new president who only celebrates reinforcement of discovery and application will wake up in 2030 running a small USC.

Guiding policy

Instruments may grow. Headcount and problem lists may not. JPL and industry are apparatus. They are not the faculty.

Coherent actions

A written “will not build” list. A JPL faculty-time firewall. Split discovery/translation budgets for quantum. A sponsorship code for student research. Hire into bottlenecks, not adjacency.

Critical uncertainties

Axis A — Federal / NASA weather. JPL and curiosity-driven agencies stay funded on scientific terms, or planetary science and campus grants become political instruments.

Axis B — What small is allowed to mean. Caltech stays a discovery shop that borrows scale, or it becomes a miniature platform university to hold its partnerships.

Scientific weather · Discovery shop

Pasadena Scale

2030

The Ginsburg Center is full of clocks, traps and graduate students arguing about noise, not product reviews. JPL is flying missions under a director who still has to get Caltech faculty on the phone. Headcount on California Boulevard has barely moved. Undergraduates still do a summer that looks like an apprenticeship on one instrument. Companies license. They do not set the weekly seminar. “Small” is still a scientific fact, not a slogan.

2040

Caltech is still the place a certain kind of scientist goes to disappear into one instrument for a decade. Citations per faculty remain absurd. A twenty-two-year-old can still be left alone with a machine that would be a national facility anywhere else. JPL and the quantum floor made money and prestige. They did not make the campus larger than the conversation.

Thin federal · Discovery shop

Quiet Institute

2030

A federal shock hits JPL — delayed missions, a retask, a headcount freeze. Campus protects its own bets: quantum, planetary science that still lives in Pasadena, the houses. From outside, Caltech looks smaller. Gift income and the endowment keep the lights on. Students notice fewer JPL badges in the cafeteria and more time with faculty who are suddenly on campus all week.

2040

Caltech is a superb, slightly isolated laboratory college. Planetary-science leadership has moved to whatever center still flies. The people who stay do extraordinary work on a quieter stage. Recruits who wanted mission-scale hardware go to Boulder, Greenbelt or a company. The Institute tells itself that isolation is focus. Sometimes it is.

Scientific weather · Platform

JPL College

2030

Money is fine. Faculty lines creep up in the names that donors recognize. Undergraduates start to feel a mission-directorate curriculum: more project codes, fewer open-ended problems, a senior thesis that already has a customer. The Ginsburg building has a company floor with better coffee and a badge reader. The faculty meeting spends half its time on space and partnership agreements.

2040

Caltech is the most elegant applied-physics contractor in the West. The faculty meeting is a portfolio review. A new assistant professor is hired to a task, not to a question. The houses still look like Caltech. The work looks like a well-run lab in a company that kept the mountains in the window.

Thin federal · Platform

Pasadena Annex

2030

Federal shocks arrive and industry writes the rescue. A Meta-class lab sets the weekly rhythm on campus: compute, internships, the seminar speaker who already has an offer letter in their pocket. The presidency is consumed by JPL advocacy in Washington. Undergraduates get extraordinary hardware and very little unassigned time. “Small” is a word used in fundraising, not in staffing.

2040

What remains is a brand, a neighborhood, and a set of well-equipped rooms. The refusal that made Caltech — the willingness to stay smaller than the opportunity — is a story told at reunion. New students arrive for the logo and the weather. They leave with a craft they could have learned in any well-funded lab that happened to have a better cafeteria.

Implications

The Board’s job in the Jayawardhana first year is not to pick a moonshot. It is to put numbers on small: student cap, faculty cap, JPL time fraction, industry-problem share. Without those numbers the attractive partnerships will make the decision.

For students · Caltech

You came here to be close to an instrument and far from a crowd. Guard both.

A new president, a new quantum building, and JPL will all try to make Caltech feel larger. Your education only works if it stays smaller than the press release.

Best case

Pasadena Scale

Ask for an instrument no larger lab would let a junior touch, and stay with it long enough to get a real measurement. Publish slowly. The scarce thing here is not a brand. It is unsupervised time on serious hardware.

Lean years

Quiet Institute

There will be fewer missions and more unclaimed faculty hours. Use them. Learn how a measurement actually gets funded — a proposal, a facility request, a “no” from a program officer — so you can do this work if JPL is no longer the hallway next door.

Mission creep

JPL College

The hardware will be excellent and the problem will already have a task number. Keep one question that does not. If your entire portfolio maps to a mission directorate, you have been staffed, not educated.

Worst case

Pasadena Annex

The logo will still be famous. The weekly work will be someone else’s roadmap. Build a portable craft — a measurement you can explain on a whiteboard without the Caltech letterhead — before you graduate.

Do this while you are here

  • Start Join a group whose weekly meeting is about a measurement, not a slide for a sponsor.
  • Start Take enough JPL or industry exposure to be bilingual — not so much that it is your only language.
  • Start Learn the instrument, not only the model that pretends to replace it.
  • Start If you are here for quantum, read the Ginsburg building as a temptation as well as a gift.
  • Stop Stop adding a side-quest that a larger university already covers. The cap is the point.
