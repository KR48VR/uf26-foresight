Board two-pager

For the President and Foundation Board

Crux

Stay the small, co-created, board-level school when AI personalises learning at scale and larger rivals can swallow the same clients — by measuring impact in the company, not in the ranking.

Diagnosis in six lines

IMD is an independent institute, not a university faculty. That is the asset: speed, a 57-person faculty, a classroom that still fits a cohort who will know each other’s names. The MBA class of 2026 is about 90 people, mid-30s, six years of work, Indian students the largest single group at around 20 percent. Custom executive education is the cash and the claim — FT number one. Bach launched Real Impact in 2025: relentlessly focused on the participant’s reality. OWP 2026 opened on competitiveness with 460-plus in Lausanne. AI Strategy and Implementation, board-level digital programmes, an Executive Master in AI and Digital Business Transformation sit on the shelf. Bach told an Indian audience that AI will change MBAs, not leadership. Fine as a sentence. Dangerous as an alibi. If personalised agents can deliver IMD’s frameworks at a tenth of the price, the only remaining scarce thing is co-creation with a specific company’s politics — the thing custom ed already is. Scale will tempt the Board to look like INSEAD. Looking like INSEAD is how IMD dies.

Guiding policy

Stay small on purpose where small is the method. Grow only the surfaces that still require a room: custom work, board programmes, company projects. Measure whether a client changed, not whether a programme filled. Keep the Swiss–Singapore–China triangle as a practiced circuit, not as three brochures.

Coherent actions

  1. An impact ledger for custom clients: pre-agreed observables inside the firm at 12 and 24 months. Publish the method even if the cases stay confidential.
  2. Cap MBA size. Ninety is a design choice. One hundred and eighty is a different school.
  3. Put every AI programme on a co-creation hook — a real organisation in the room — or kill it. Frameworks without a host are INSEAD’s catalogue war, not IMD’s.
  4. A written rule for the Shenzhen and Singapore nodes: they exist to bring Asian reality into Lausanne work, not to become regional IMDs.

Start

A sitting-manager room standard: every module ends in a decision the participant owned. Cap cohort growth against that room.

Stop

Content-house modules with thin peers. Video as the year.

Go

The executive room of people who still run something. Adapt the case; keep the sitting manager.

Full report

What IMD is

A Swiss independent business school that lives on closeness to the firm. Lausanne is the house. Singapore is a month-long Future Lab for the MBA and a campus for Asian exec-ed. Shenzhen is an office, not a third school — unless neglect makes it one. Competitiveness research (Arturo Bris and the World Competitiveness Center) is the thought-leadership spine.

Forces that actually move this institution

  • AI as unbundler of open programmes. Frameworks travel. Co-creation does not. The first casualty, if the Board is careless, is the open-enrolment catalogue, not the custom work.
  • Scale envy. INSEAD’s 1,100 MBAs and Harvard’s brand will always look like winning. They are a different sport.
  • Geopolitics on a three-node map. Swiss neutrality is an asset until it is a fantasy. Singapore and Shenzhen only work if faculty still fly and clients still mix.
  • Talent mix. India’s weight in the MBA is a strength and a concentration. A school this small cannot afford a single-country classroom.
“AI will change MBAs, not leadership.” — David Bach, World Government Summit, February 2026

Crux

Make “impact inside the firm” the operating system — so that AI tools deepen co-creation instead of turning IMD into a prettier content shop. Size, measurement, and a refusal to regionalise the nodes are the addressable moves.

Kernel Reconstruction, September 2026 — not IMD’s published plan

Diagnosis

IMD is a co-creation shop whose new AI catalogue can either serve the shop or replace it with scale it cannot win.

Stay small where small is method. Measure clients, not rankings. Keep Asia as a circuit, not a franchise.

Coherent actions

Impact ledger. MBA cap. Host-organisation rule for AI programmes. Written purpose for Singapore and Shenzhen. Kill open programmes that agents can already teach.

2040

A holds · B split

Swiss core

2030

Clients still come to Lausanne. Asia nodes thin because flying faculty and mixed classrooms get harder. The MBA is more European. Custom work remains real and narrower in geography. A participant who needed the China or ASEAN room builds it themselves after the programme.

2040

Excellent and less world. A regional atelier with a global memory. INSEAD and CEIBS take the participants who used to treat the triangle as the point. Competitiveness research still travels; the classroom mix does not.

A unbundled · B triangle

Pretty catalogue

2030

Geography still works — people can still fly. Product does not. Agents teach the open programmes; custom work starts to look like a longer open programme with better coffee. Faculty time tilts toward campus theatre because theatre is easier to staff than a week inside a difficult firm.

2040

A travel brand attached to content. The FT custom ranking is a lagging trophy. Clients who needed co-creation have gone in-house or to a consultancy that will stay on site.

A unbundled · B split

Small hotel

2030

Both the method and the map go. Open programmes shrink to what an agent can already teach. Custom work becomes occasional. Singapore and Shenzhen are addresses. The MBA quietly grows to look busy.

2040

A well-run Swiss campus that used to set a standard. The view is intact. The work moved to wherever a firm can still get a small team to sit with its actual politics.

Implications

An impact ledger and an MBA cap help in every world except “small hotel.” Copying INSEAD’s volume helps in none of them. Designed small is the strategy, not the consolation prize.

A bad strategy would be: add AI to every brochure, grow the MBA to look serious, and treat Shenzhen as a market rather than a circuit.
