Board two-pager

For the Vice-Chancellor and Council

Crux

Make the collegiate tutorial certify scarce judgment after AI cheapens the essay — without letting 36 unevenly endowed colleges drift into a two-tier university, and without becoming a UK-state AI facility that happens to own some fifteenth-century rooms.

Diagnosis in six lines

Oxford is a federal university whose undergraduate education still runs on a labour-intensive tutorial that the rest of the sector abandoned as too expensive. That is now an asset, not an anachronism — if the essay the tutor marks is not a model draft with a college stamp. Tracey has said the quiet part: a thousand-year history of close teaching is “more essential now than ever.” Meanwhile the centre is building a different university: Schwarzman Centre open October 2025 (£185 million), AI@Oxford Research under Nigel Shadbolt, ChatGPT Edu, a £4 billion campaign in the 2025–30 plan, Ellison Institute doctoral training in the fundamentals of AI, a BOLD lab with UCL and Imperial on cheaper UK-made models. Those are real. They do not automatically preserve the tutorial. College endowments run from about £1.23 million per student at Magdalen to £2,400 at Green Templeton. A student can leave with the same degree and a radically different education. UK policy is taxing the international fees that cross-subsidise the whole arrangement, and CAS issuance across the sector is down sharply for 2026 entry. Oxford is richer than Cardiff. It is not rich enough to ignore the levy, the visa office, or the college that cannot fund contact hours the rich colleges treat as air.

Guiding policy

Treat the tutorial as the product and AI fluency as hygiene. Publish what “close teaching” now means when a model can write the weekly essay. Use central philanthropy and the campaign to underwrite tutorial density in poor colleges, not only new buildings and AI chairs. Keep compute and model partnerships switchable. Do not let the science and policy labs set the meaning of an Oxford degree.

Coherent actions

  1. Define a university-wide tutorial standard for the AI decade: at least one piece of work per term that cannot be sat by a model — viva, problem class, lab, translation on the page, unseen. Publish college compliance, not a style guide.
  2. Create a Collegiate Teaching Floor funded from the campaign and a slice of rich-college surplus: ring-fenced contact hours and graduate-tutor pay in the bottom third of colleges by endowment per student.
  3. Cap deep vendor lock-in. ChatGPT Edu is a tool. Switchability is a Council metric. BOLD and sovereign compute are the hedge, not a branding exercise.
  4. Put the Schwarzman Centre, OII, and Institute for Ethics in AI on the same assessment question as Physics and PPE: what human work is being certified, and who sees it.

Start

A university-wide tutorial standard for the AI decade: one piece of work per term that cannot be outsourced. A published tutorial-load floor.

Stop

Growing overseas volume as if that were the college. Soft finals behind a beautiful wall.

Go

The tutorial and the college. Adapt the assignment; keep the hour that can still fail you.

Full report

What Oxford is

Oxford is a collegiate research university of about 26,800 students as of 1 December 2025: 12,460 undergraduates, 13,755 graduates, plus a thin visiting layer. Thirty-six colleges and a handful of halls house most of them and still deliver a large share of undergraduate teaching. The University itself holds about £4.2 billion of endowment; the colleges together reported £7.1 billion at 31 July 2025. That split is the governance fact. Council can open a humanities centre. It cannot make St John’s spend like Mansfield.

The public monuments of 2025–26 are easy to list. The Stephen A. Schwarzman Centre for the Humanities opened to academics in October 2025 and was opened by the King in July 2026. It co-locates seven faculties with the Oxford Internet Institute, the Institute for Ethics in AI, and a new Bodleian humanities library. Sir Nigel Shadbolt became chair of AI@Oxford Research in November 2025. The Vice-Chancellor’s Colloquium added an AI theme in Hilary 2026 and put more than 500 students through it in the Schwarzman building. Oxford and UCL won a share of up to £60 million in UK public money to build cheaper, more open models; Jakob Foerster leads the BOLD lab with Imperial in the mix. The Dieter Schwarz Stiftung renewed its OII partnership in September 2026. None of that is the crux. The crux is whether a federal medieval-modern hybrid can still sell a scarce human education when the essay is cheap and the state is a harsher customer.

Forces that actually move this institution

  • The tutorial as an expensive asset. Close teaching survived here because colleges paid for it. AI makes the weekly essay cheap to produce and expensive to examine well. Tracey’s 2025 oration named the risk of students “over-using” AI and reached for the tutorial as the answer. Reaching is not redesigning.
  • College inequality. Magdalen on the order of £1.23 million of endowment per student; Green Templeton about £2,400. Same degree. Different capacity to staff a human room. A campaign that only builds centres will widen that.
  • UK higher-education arithmetic. Frozen-then-raised home fees, a proposed international student levy from 2028/29, tighter visas, CAS issuance down about 27 percent across partner networks for September 2026. Oxford is not Cardiff. It still lives on overseas taught-graduate fees more than the brochure admits.
  • State AI industrial policy. DSIT, UKRI, AIRR compute, “sovereign” models. Oxford can take the money and remain a university, or become a node in a national lab network that happens to matriculate PPE students.
  • Geopolitics as admissions and gifts. Schwarzman, Ellison, Dieter Schwarz, DeepMind scholarships, China as a graduate market. Each donor and each visa rule is a different claim on what Oxford is for.
“We must remember that our 1,000-year history of the tutorial and close-teaching is more essential now than ever.” — Irene Tracey, Oration, October 2025

Crux

The addressable difficulty is this: convert the tutorial from a cherished habit into a designed certification of judgment the model cannot sit — and finance that standard across poor as well as rich colleges — while the centre is being pulled into national AI infrastructure. A new institute does not touch it. A ChatGPT licence does not touch it. A teaching floor and a public assessment standard do.

Kernel Reconstruction, September 2026 — not Oxford’s published plan

Diagnosis

Oxford solved “look ancient and score modern.” The remaining problem is product definition: a federal university whose only scarce undergraduate technology is close human teaching, running on unequal college balance sheets, under a state that wants both AI capacity and fewer foreign students.

Guiding policy

Protect tutorial density as the brand. Use the campaign to floor the poor colleges. Take sovereign-AI money that does not buy the question. Keep vendors replaceable. Let Schwarzman house the humanities; do not let it become the alibi for leaving collections as they were in 2019.

Coherent actions

University-wide AI-era tutorial standard with a public scoreboard. Collegiate Teaching Floor. Vendor-switch metric. Shared assessment question across OII, Ethics in AI, and the old faculties. Stop announcing chairs until the last three have graduate-tutor hours attached in weak colleges.

Critical uncertainties

Axis A — Britain as an open talent and research hub. Visas, levy, and security rules stay livable for a two-facing university, or they tighten Oxford into a more British, more security-aligned campus.

Axis B — The degree as signal. A residential Oxford degree stays scarce because employers still cannot fake the judgment a tutorial certifies, or credentials commoditise into model-mediated portfolios plus a college tie.

A open · B scarce

Collegiate republic

2030

A first-year in PPE or Physics still writes a weekly piece, but at least one item a term is now a viva, a translation on the page, or a problem class the model is not allowed into. Magdalen and Mansfield both meet a published contact-hour floor paid from the campaign, so the poor college is no longer improvising tutors on leftover fees. International graduates still arrive from the US and from China, even if the taught-master’s intake is a little smaller. Employers who used to skim the college name start asking which papers were sat unseen.

2040

Oxford is slightly smaller in taught-graduate mass and more distinctive as a result. A hiring panel in Boston or Singapore can still tell an Oxford tutorial transcript from a stacked-certificate portfolio because the viva record travels with it. The Schwarzman Centre is a workplace for the humanities, not a substitute for collections. The UK uses the university as a diplomatic object — visiting premiers still want the hall photograph — but Council has not let that job rewrite the degree.

A open · B cheap

Platform college

2030

Collections and finals have quietly become take-home plus an oral that lasts twelve minutes. ChatGPT Edu and a college ‘AI fluency’ badge do most of what the weekly essay used to do. A student can leave with a first and a dining-club network without ever having been in a room where the model was off. Research hotels — Ellison, BOLD, the clinical schools — thrive because they do not depend on undergraduate contact hours.

2040

The valuable Oxford is the institutes, the hospitals, and the press. The undergraduate college is a prestige wrapper: a three-year residential brand around a national skills and research platform. Tutors still exist in the rich colleges as a boutique. Weak colleges have become halls of residence with a marking service. Foreign faculties treat the BA as a signal of having been selected at 17, not of having been taught at 21.

A tight · B scarce

National cloister

2030

Visa refusals and a security-first Britain thin the Chinese and some American taught-graduate pipelines. Tutorials get more British and, in a few science and area-studies papers, more clearance-gated. The degree is scarce because clinical access, official data, and local language work cannot be offshored. A student who wanted a two-passport career starts arranging the second door in year two.

2040

Excellent and less internationally legible. Talent that wanted a two-passport career leaves earlier, or never applies. Rich colleges still look like themselves; weak colleges look like underfunded halls attached to a national lab. Oxford remains first in the UK and second in every conversation that used to include Harvard and Beida as peers rather than counterparts.

A tight · B cheap

Heritage franchise

2030

Both uniqueness and openness go. The University administers national modules, licences the crest for overseas teaching centres, and lets the brand tour. A student can sit most of the BA as a promptable portfolio plus three weeks of formal hall. Building works and donor naming continue; contact hours do not.

2040

Research excellence concentrates in a few institutes and companies that happen to use Oxford stationery. The collegiate university is a hospitality layer over a skills-and-science agency. Tourists still queue at the Radcliffe Camera. Hiring panels stop treating the BA as scarce.

Implications

Council does not need to pick a world. It needs a policy that is not ruined by three of the four. A teaching floor and switchable vendors help in every world except “heritage franchise,” where nothing Council does is the binding constraint. Schwarzman and BOLD help most in “collegiate republic” and “national cloister.” They are wasted motion in “platform college” unless they come with exclusive data and harder exams.

A bad strategy would be: announce another AI centre, renew the OpenAI deal as if it were a strategy, leave collections as they were in 2019 with an honour-code paragraph, and let college inequality do the rest.

For students · Oxford

The college will feed you and put you in a beautiful room. It will not automatically teach you anything a model cannot already do.

You were admitted to a university that still pretends a weekly essay is a conversation. That is only true if someone in the room can tell your work from a well-prompted draft — and if your college can still pay for that someone. Choose the room, not the crest.

Best case for you

Collegiate republic

Harder tutorials. Real vivas. A transcript that still sorts you from a prompt notebook. Worth the weather.

Comfortable trap

Platform college

Everything is assisted. Your collections look like everyone else’s collections. Employers start asking what you did with the camera off.

Narrow excellence

National cloister

Fewer overseas doors. Deeper British and security-cleared ones. If you wanted a two-passport career, plan the second door yourself.

Worst case

Heritage franchise

You collected a brand. The scarce jobs went to people who built things outside the tutorial.

Do this while you are here

  • Start Collect at least one piece of work per term that bans the model in the room. If your course has none, that is information about the course.
  • Start Read the college accounts. Endowment per student is not a vibe. It is whether your tutorials will still exist in 2029.
  • Go Use Schwarzman, OII, and the labs as instruments, not as a substitute for a department that still marks hard.
  • Go Keep a working relationship in a second system. Oxford’s value was two-facing. So is yours.
  • Stop Stop sitting a collection the model can sit. Demand the viva, the unseen, the page.
